Cllrs. Kiely and McSweeney opened the discussion by highlighting their concerns around tree

management in the City and County.

A lengthy discussion took place on the LCCC Tree Policy and on all aspects of tree

management in the city and county. Members outlined multiple issues they have encountered

regarding tree management and questioned if the Tree Management policy is adequate and is

being implemented properly. Issues included,

•

Health and safety issues,

•

Tree encroachment of property and footpaths,

•

Trees blocking light to homes and businesses

•

Risk of storm damage.

•

Trees on private property and the Cost to property owners

•

Resources to carry out works

•

Ash Die-back

Councillors highlighted the need for more tree management works to be carried out before the

end of the year to address these issues. Senior Engineer Aidan Finn responded to the specific

concerns and outlined the inadequacy of the annual budget allocated to tree management as a

key factor in successfully implementing the Tree Policy. He advised that roadside trees are the

responsibility of the Roads department, mentioning specifically Section 70 of the Roads Act and

these incidents need to be reported to the Roads Team. Lighting is also a responsibility of the

Roads department – the Tree Policy is not applicable here. Following further discussion, the

members agreed the following recommendation.

**Having regard to the need for more active tree management, this Council seeks a**

**supplementary allocation in 2025 to address urgent cases and an increase in the 2026**

**allocation to €300,000.**

**Item 3** **–** **Notice of Motion - Cllr D. McSweeney** ***(referred from July Council Meeting) -*** **I will move at**

**the next Meeting of Limerick City and County Council that we examine the rollout of a Community**

**Contracts Grant Scheme in advance of budget 2026.**

I will move at the next Meeting of Limerick City and County Council that we examine the rollout

of a Community Contracts Grant Scheme in advance of budget 2026.

Cllr McSweeney outlined his motion and added that there is a Community Grant scheme

running successfully in Cork County Council. Communities are being awarded funding for all

the work they carry out. Grants from €1,500 to €20,000 are given to Cork communities. There

are some grant schemes in Limerick, but they are not adequate to meet the costs to community

groups.

Response –

‘The Environment and Climate Action department issue a number of grants to communities

across Limerick. There is a community grant available for grass cutting where communities

apply and receive a grant dependent on the amount of grass that is being maintained. There is

also a grant for burial ground maintenance and both grants are reviewed on an annual basis to

ensure that communities receive an appropriate grant amount. There are also grants available

under the Communities Climate Action Fund and also Going for Gold which are administered

by the Environment and Climate Action Department. There may be a case for the Council to
---
review community grants in general across the organisation to see the level of grants that are

currently available and whether there is a need to provide further grant aid.’

A discussion took place around the work of communities particularly Tidy Towns groups and the

budgetary implications of the motion.

**A majority of members indicated their support for the motion and the motion was**

**recommended.**

**Item 4** **–** **Discussion in advance of Public Consultation - New Cemeteries Byelaws** **–** **Brian Henry,**

**Senior Executive Technician, Cemeteries Operations.**

Mr. Henry presented on the review of the Cemeteries Byelaws which is underway. The currently

Byelaws date from 2015 and require updating in line with the Limerick City and County Council

Burial Ground Strategy. The review aims to clarify the rights and responsibilities of all key

stakeholders.

Several issues have arisen since the last bye-laws were issued;

 Instances of non-adherence by key stakeholders in relation to the bye-laws

 The actions and/or sanctions available in the event of breaches of bye-laws

 The management of those cemeteries designated as lawn cemeteries, and addressing

non-compliance with those bye-laws which govern them

 Interactions between Limerick City and County Council staff and the public

MyPoint Consultation opens October 2025 for six weeks. The Terms of Reference will be

published on MyPoint. Participants will be asked whether they are satisfied with current bye-

laws or wish to see changes. The consultation will be advertised via social media, LCCC

website, email, and newspapers.

Target timeline: Bye-laws ready for ratification in Q2/Q3 2026.

Mr. Finn clarified that the consultation relates to the current bye-laws and seeks input from

members, the public, and other stakeholders.

Members raised a number of issues with regard to the current Byelaws; Maintenance,

Monuments (including oversized/unauthorised), Health and safety, Availability of grave spaces,

Furniture/seating on graves, Enforcement of current bye-laws

On enforcement, Mr. Finn noted that while enforcement occurs, current powers under the bye-

laws need to be strengthened, which is why stakeholder input is critical at this stage.

**Action Agreed:**

Presentation will be circulated to all members. The PPN will be contacted directly to ensure

relevant organisations are consulted throughout the process.

**Item 5** **–** **Community Climate Action Fund, relaunch 2025** **–** **Dr. Darren Barry, Community Climate**

**Action Officer, Environment & Climate Action.**

Dr. Barry presented on the launch of Phase 2 of the Community Climate Action Programme. A

total of €811,000 has been allocated to Limerick City and County Council to partner with

communities in Limerick to build low-carbon communities in a structured way.

Funding categories: Small-scale projects: < €20,000 (10% of projects), Medium-scale projects:

€20,000–€50,000 (40% of projects), Large-scale projects: €51,000–€100,000 (50% of projects)

Key requirements:

Community groups must be not-for-profit, registered, hold tax clearance, and have a bank

account. Projects must be completed within 18 months and 100% funding will be provided.

Programme themes: Community/Energy, Travel, Food and Waste, Shopping and Recycling,

Local Climate and Environmental Action. Timeline: Launch: 21 October 2025, Successful

groups notified: 31 March 2026.

Committee members welcomed the launch and discussed Phase 1 outcomes and other

climate action projects. Key discussion points:

Communications Protocols: Concerns were raised about communication with communities

regarding application process and results. Dr. Barry noted that Phase 1 was subject to strict

Department instructions before sign-off, but improvements are expected in Phase 2.

Active Travel Scheme Maintenance Costs: Members expressed frustration regarding the

medium of long-term maintenance of Active Travel Schemes. Mr. K Lehane advised that while

the Department funds capital works, maintenance costs fall on Local Authorities. He

acknowledged the deterioration of some schemes and stressed the need for maintenance

funding. To date, over €50 million has been spent on Active Travel measures. He stated that

future Part 8 proposals should consider full life-cycle funding.

**Action Agreed:**

The Council will write to the Department of Transport requesting maintenance funding for

Active Travel measures.
