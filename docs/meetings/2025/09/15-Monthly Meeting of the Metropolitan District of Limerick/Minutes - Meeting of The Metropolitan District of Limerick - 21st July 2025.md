**4.Limerick City Centre**

(a) **Presentation on Night Time Economy Plan**

The Night Time Economy Advisor, with the aid of a presentation, gave an update to Members on

the Night Time Economy Plan for Limerick City. He referred to the Plan as a clear vision for the

night economy in Limerick, which aims to support businesses, residents and visitors.

The Night Time Economy Advisor outlined the following plans for Twilight Thursday, noting how

this initiative has grown month on month since its initial launch:

• Two editions of Twilight Thursday had been successfully implemented with the next edition to

take place on 31st July.

• Partnerships had been acquired with Uber and FreeNow which would make the city centre

more accessible for Twilight Thursday dates.

• Implementation of national Night Time Economy Initiative ‘Café Lates’, which would see later

opening and activation of cafes within the city centre.

• Promotion of “have a night out on” The Limerick Gift Card campaign began, with plans for a

larger marketing campaign to follow, subject to sufficient budget.

• Launch of Night Time Walking Routes with the promotion and animation of The Three Bridges

route.

• A cohesive brand identity and message for Limerick at Night and Twilight Thursday had been

created.

He also outlined that in the coming months it is intended to launch the national Night Time

Christmas.

***night activity in our local economy.***

**REPLY**: The Minister for Housing, Local Government and Heritage, James Browne, confirmed that

seating licences or supports to businesses that pay a living wage. While the Council

supports outdoor dining through fee waivers and infrastructure improvements, the

adoption of living wage policies falls under national pay frameworks. As such, the Council

does not currently have discretion to implement wage-based incentives or conditions for

licence rebates.

**16.Question submitted by Councillor S. Hickey-O’Mara**

***I will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District:***

***(a) How often was the Council contacted to remove roadkill in the Metropolitan area over the***

***past 5 years.***

***(b) If any roadkill blackspots have been identified by the Council as a result.***

***(c) Whether the Council records the species of animal when removing roadkill in the***

***Metropolitan area.***

***(d) Were there any significant increases/decreases in the number of animals removed per month***

***i.e. are there times of the year that saw an increase or decrease in roadkill.***

***(e) How is this roadkill disposed of once removed.***

**REPLY**:

(a) The Council’s complaints system does not have a service option for small dead animals which

can be searched and result in definitive data, however the Customer Service Team has

conducted a search of the CRM system for the years 2020 to 2025 which shows approximately

101 reports of dead animals on roads requiring removal. The actual amount of roadkill is

probably higher.

(b) No particular roadkill blackspots have been identified but they tend to occur on heavily

trafficked routes.

(c) The 101 reports referred to above include 12 reports of dead dogs, 8 reports of dead cats, 1

report of a dead bird, 28 reports of dead foxes and 7 reports of other animals of unrecorded

species.

(d) Roadkill occurs throughout the year, no significant increases/decreases per month have been

noted.

(e) Roadkill is disposed of as general waste which is processed by a waste management company.

**18.Question submitted by Councillor P. Reale**

***I will ask at the next Meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick for an update on what***

***discussions are ongoing with the*** ***Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage***

***in*** ***relation to the wind down of the Economic & Social Intervention Fund in Limerick and what***

***discussions are happening in relation to identifying an alternative funding stream to support***

***these communities who benefit from these funds.***

**REPLY**: The Limerick Regeneration Framework Implementation Plan (LRFIP) was launched in 2013,

which was a ten-year programme to deliver on social, economic and physical objectives.

Post-2023, the Regeneration, Sports & Recreation Directorate has continued to deliver on

the objectives outlined within the Plan with the continued support of the Department of

Housing, Local Government and Heritage (DHLGH).

The DHLGH gave guidance via letter on the 9th of May 2025, that the Economic and Social

Intervention Fund (ESIF) funded by their Department, is set to wind down with a final

tranche of funding to be granted in 2028. The Regeneration, Sports & Recreation

Directorate replied via letter on the 19th of May 2025 to appeal this decision. The

Regeneration, Sports & Recreation Directorate continues to regularly meet and engage

with Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage officials regarding ESIF and

on developing and progressing new capital projects. Staff have also briefed the Oireachtas

Members on the status of the Economic & Social Intervention Fund who in turn will engage

with the relevant Minister in this regard.

Within the Regeneration, Sports & Recreation Directorate, the social regeneration team

works with agencies, service providers, and community groups on an ongoing basis to

identify opportunities for collaboration, coordination, and funding.

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Donoghue, seconded by Councillor Hickey-

O’Mara and agreed.

In proposing the Motion, Councillor Donoghue emphasised the importance of implementing a

mixed tenure policy that would ensure the creation of a city that is equitable and sustainable.

**REPLY**:

The Council appraises residential schemes in accordance with the policy provisions of the Limerick

Development Plan and Housing Strategy. In this regard in accordance with Objectives set out in

Chapter 4 of the Limerick Development Plan, the Council seeks to support a range of housing

options in residential schemes across the City. At an early stage and part of the pre-planning

consultation, applicants are encouraged to incorporate a mix of tenure in residential developments

and to bring forward schemes of a high quality that will both attract and offer choice to new

residents to the City Centre.

In accordance with the Planning and Development Act 2000 (as amended), an applicant is required

to meet the provisions of Part V of the Act (proportion of a scheme to be provided for social and/or

affordable housing provision) and this requirement is a condition for any permission. An applicant

may bring forward a fully private and owner occupied scheme subject to this Part V requirement.

Alternatively, an applicant may seek to bring forward a mixed tenure scheme involving a blend of

ownership, private rental, social-rental, and affordable housing (both affordable sale and affordable

rental) integrated within the same development.
---
16

While Part V of the Planning and Development Act, 2000 has express provision for a planning

condition to be attached in respect of a particular tenure, i.e. social and affordable housing, there

is no legislative provision to extend this to restrict tenure across a full scheme. To do this could

affect property rights and it would not be possible to enforce through planning provisions.

However, the Council through the Housing Section and in implementing ‘Housing for all’

administers a range of Government supported scheme to promote a mix tenure approach. In

applying a structure approach, for large-scale housing developments (over 40 units) proposed to

LCCC that are seeking State subvention to deliver social and/or affordable above Part V provision

for example, the following approach has been agreed with the Elected Members of Limerick City

and County Council:

A minimum of 20% private tenure - presumption in favour of private sale subject to viability

A minimum of 50% private affordable tenure - presumption in favour of affordable sale subject to

viability

A maximum of 30% social-rental tenure, including rightsizing and Part V properties

For acquisitions, leases, or turnkeys with fewer than 40 units, where social-rental tenure is

proposed, a thorough pre-assessment process is followed:

• A review of housing needs in the area for the proposed property types, with consultation via

the I-house system and confirmation from the Housing Support Services Allocations unit of the

waiting list for the bed type proposed.

• An evaluation of the level of social-rental housing (including Housing Assistance Payment [HAP])

in the area to avoid an overconcentration of any one tenure type. **The approach of the Housing**

**Directorate is no more than 30% on an Electoral Division within the City and for settlements**

**outside the City the built up town boundary is used.**

• An analysis of the HP Pobal Deprivation Index for the area to ensure that areas classified as

‘extremely disadvantaged’ or ‘very disadvantaged’ are not recommended for progression.

• In relation to acquisitions we acknowledge that acquisition funding is limited in 2025. However

should a purchase of a house be required for a particular case, a check is undertaken to ensure

that no first-time buyers are bidding. The Housing Directorate seeks confirmation from the

vendor/Estate Agent that first-time buyers are not involved in bidding for the property. If they

are, the Housing Directorate will not support the acquisition of the unit.

Overall, the implementation of Limerick City and County Council's mixed-tenure policy has been

successful in fostering balanced and integrated communities. The structured approach to both

large and small-scale developments has generally achieved the intended goals of avoiding mono-

tenure areas and overconcentration of social-rental housing. However, as with any policy, there

have been a small number of cases where challenges have arisen, but these are being actively

addressed to ensure the long-term success and sustainability of the policy.

Members requested that a Briefing on the Council’s Mixed Tenure Policy would be arranged.
