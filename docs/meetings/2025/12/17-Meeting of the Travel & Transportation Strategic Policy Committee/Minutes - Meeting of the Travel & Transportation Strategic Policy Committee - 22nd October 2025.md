**Item 1: Minutes:**

The Minutes of the Meeting of the Travel and Transportation Strategic Policy Committee held on

the 10 of June 2025 were proposed by Cllr. E. O’Donovan and seconded by Cllr. O’Donoghue.

**Item 2: Think Before You Park Campaign’** **- Update by Mrs. Maureen Browne, Social Inclusion**

**PPN.**

Mrs. Browne delivered a presentation on the ‘Think Before You Park campaign’, established in

2004 and outlined the purposes of the group. Audits undertaken in the Access Awareness project,

launched in September 2022 identified the issues across all communities caused by parking on

footpaths.

The Chair thanked Mrs. Browne for the presentation adding that it is a very effective campaign.

Cllr. J. O’Donoghue thanked Mrs. Browne adding that this is the first he has seen of the campaign

adding that it is a great initiative.

Mrs. Browne added that people cannot jump off footpath to avoid cars and wheelie bins parked

in their paths. She added that planning has a lot to do with it as there is no planning for parking

and respect would go a long way for all concerned.

Cllr. O’Donovan complimented the excellent work. She added that the issue is going to increase

due to the growing population Planning and policy leaving us down, guidelines should be law.

She did a walkability audit walk with Mrs. Browne and suggested that there should be a disability

audit for every project especially Active Travel projects.

Cllr. S. Hartigan highlighted the cars parked on footpaths daily around UL and suggested that

providing more parking is not a solution, awareness and enforcement is critical. Cllr. Ryan

advised that message is getting through and has seen improvements in Ballylanders, where car

spaces are marked out.

Mrs. Browne advised that there is no issue in the wide Main Street in Ballylanders however, once

out of the main street, there is parking on footpaths in Kilfinane and Galbally Road as drivers

want to park right outside visiting premises. Cllr. Gavan also highlighted the increasing number

of cars and wheelie bins on footpaths in her area.

Cllr. Secas also welcomed Ms. Liddy and thanked Mr. McGrath for the support to date. She added

that a holistic approach is required as well as better planning, a change in behaviour and

awareness.

Mr. Tobin asked Mrs. Browne if she would attend the next Road Safety Working Together Group

(RSWTG) and if her campaign be included in the updates. He also asked if she could feed into the

next walkability audit with Toby Goodwin, Active Travel.

**Item 5: EV Implementation Stage and Update on Progress to date** **–John O’Leary, A/S.E.E.**

Mr. O’Leary delivered a presentation on EV Charging Infrastructure implementation and progress

to date, outlining the background policies and objectives and detailing LCCC’s EV strategy.

The Chair thanked Mr. O’Leary and asked for a timeline in the Kilmallock area. In response Mr.

O’Leary advised that the main civils infrastructure is ready and the chargers are scheduled for

initial installation before Christmas with anticipated power-on from ESB Networks in January.

The Chair also enquired about the sale of EV vehicles. Mr. O’Leary advised that EV sales had

dropped in 2024, however there has been a move upwards in 2025.

Cllr. Conway enquired if the local authority is under pressure to shift the fleet to EV and asked

how long it takes to charge a car and if there will be supervision.

Cllr. J. O’ Donoghue stated that it is difficult to plan around modal shift and it is mostly hybrids

being purchased. He asked about the cost, as cost needs to be considered and does it make sense

financially nationally or locally. Trade in value is not great for EV’s and the world is evolving. EV’s

are not suitable for larger vehicles and HGVS.

In response, Mr. O’Leary advised that overstay penalties will be imposed by the Charge Point

Operator for prolonged parking after the charging is complete and byelaws will also apply in

relation disc parking zones where chargers are to be located.

In response to Cllr. O’ Donoghue, Mr. O’Leary advised that the costs of project are funded

through ZEVI and operations and maintenance on the concession contract will be cost neutral to

the Council. Regarding heavy goods vehicles, technology is very much developing at present but

most likely in the direction of hydrogen or HVO (Hydro-treated Vegetable Oil) biofuel.

Mr. O’Leary added that many of the gaps in the map for EV charging are down to grid capacity

issues and waiting on upgrades to the system by EirGrid and ESB Networks over the coming years.

Cllr. Hartigan queried the target status of our 2025 Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation

(AFIR) requirements. In response, Mr. O’Leary advised that that there are approx. 130 public

charging sites in Limerick and that LCCC are projected to meet its AFIR targets for 2025 and will

then focus on the 2030 targets.

Mr. Tobin stated that it is a game changer and asked how does the LCCC strategy fits into this

and if the LIHAF road model fits into other areas. He also queried on-street parking in the city,

charging cables on footpaths and local charging hubs.

Cllr. Secas advised she moved from EV to diesel because of cost on her ESB bill. Cllr. Gavan

echoed Cllr. Secas comments and asked if there is any EV charger planned for Castleconnell area.

Mr. O’Leary advised that the EV charger for Castletroy will be at the playground and Annacotty

may be included in the next phase of charging sites subject to grid capacity. Castleconnell was

reviewed recently and grid capacity issues still constrain the optimum siting locations for a public

charger.

Cllr. Hartigan shared positive posts on Google regarding the cost benefit of EV’s. Cllr. E. Ryan

highlighted the negative costs of EV’s. John O Donoghue, referenced negative costs, trade in

value and cost of disposing of batteries.

Mr. O’Leary added that the Mungret LIHAF model is repeatable in terms of delivery of residential

neighborhood charging. Regarding charging for homes without off-street parking, the current

solution is to use neighborhood charging hubs due to legal, health & safety and insurance issues

regarding trailing EV charging cables across footpaths in the public realm. Most of the feedback

from people on EV Charging has been positive.

**Item 3: Transport Oriented Development (TOD) in Limerick** **–** **Update by Mr. Carmel Brislane,**

**S.E.P.**

Ms. Carmel Brislane delivered a presentation on the urban development approach that

integrates land uses with access to transport and concentrates housing, employment, and leisure
---
5

space near public transport nodes, such as rail and bus stations. She outlined the NDP submission

for €335 million request to deliver two transformative Transit Oriented Development (TOD)

infrastructure packages in Limerick City

The Chair thanked Ms. Brislane for the presentation and asked if locations were identified. In

response, Ms. Brislane stated that locations are yet to be confirmed, Bus Connects and Rail will

be the main drivers for this project.

Cllr. O’ Donovan thanked Ms. Brislane for the interesting presentation. She queried if TOD sites

are focussed around housing, industry or health care. One is needed in Dooradoyle and health

care facilities should be prioritised.

Cllr. Kilcoyne agreed with Cllr. O’Donovan and noticed the absence of Patrickswell, Adare,

Rosbrien and Castletroy on map of train sites. He added that a new Roundabout is going in at

Lucey’s cross and Bus Connects to turn at Roundabout to go back into village of Patrickswell.

Cllr. Gavan raised questions in relation to Annacotty, which has a railway station and 3000 people

working in small business in the industrial estate.

In response to Cllr. O’Donovan, Ms. Brislane stated that the TOD’s are in mixed use development

sites. In reply to Cllr. Kilcoyne, she confirmed that the TOD is specific for the five Cities or a

metropolitan town, i.e. Limerick city and Shannon town where there is high capacity and

frequency of bus/rail services. It is hugely dependent on Bus Connects. When she receives

report from Dept. of Transport, she will have more information

**Item 4: Limerick City Centre Transport Plan** **–** **Update by Mr. Hugh McGrath, S.E.**

Mr. Hugh McGrath delivered a presentation on the Limerick City Centre Transport plan

outlining the objectives.

The Chair thanked Mr. McGrath and opened the floor to questions.

Mr. Golden asked what the Transport plan encompasses now and looked for clarification on the

transport plan scope to include deliveries.

Cllr. O’Donovan stated that there is a very heavy emphasis on busses without the basic

infrastructure in city. She also enquired about carshare.

In response to Mr. Tobin in relation to timeline of key bus corridors, Mr. McGrath stated that the

Transport plan must come first, we will be pushing to get key corridors funded in the coming

years.

In relation to Car Share, Mr. McGrath advised Limerick are hoping to have a new provider soon.

Bike share, bike and e-scooter scheme is to be put in place in 2026 in conjunction with the NTA

and we will not lose the TFI bike scheme until a new scheme is in place.
---
6

In response to Mr. Golden, Mr. McGrath advised that the plan should be about prioritising

movement of people and goods. We are working on the delivery strategy, which will be about

assessment of delivery needs, with appropriate parking management and work has already

started on this with other cities. While this will be addressed in the transport plan, we have

begun data collection and have commenced a review of parking demand\pricing in line with

other cities and to ensure the best outcome to facilitate optimal use of road space to the benefit

of the city.

Cllr. Secas appreciates the work, understands one is going for planning for Park and Ride, and

asked which site it will be. Mr. McGrath advise that the ambition is to get all four strategic sites

advanced (Mackey, Raheen, Coonagh and Ballysimon) however unable to confirm which site is

likely to be advanced first as all have some work to do. It is hoped to progress at least one site to

planning this year.

Cllr. Gavan understood it was the Mackey scheme which was to go through due to the Ryder Cup

and complimented the work in Annacotty Business Park.

Mr. McGrath confirmed that there is engagement between LCCC, NTA and TII however not quite

there yet due to traffic considerations and Limerick City and County Council has to work in

conjunction with TII to ensure any development demonstrates a positive impact on the traffic

issues at Junction 29. The Mackey Park and Ride for Ryder Cup is a different concept but will be

a good pilot to showcase the overall principle of park and ride.

Ms. Liddy informed the members that she was very happy to be back in Limerick and thanked

the members for the warm welcome. There are a lot of challenges, and the main thing is to get

funding going forward.

Following a discussion by the members, it was agreed to change the time of future SPC meetings

to 2:15p.m.

This concluded the business of the meeting.
