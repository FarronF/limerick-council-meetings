***Background to Government's Turnkey Delivery Stream***

• The acquisition of turnkey properties is an established Government social

housing delivery mechanism operated by local authorities in accordance with

Department of Housing Circular 31/2019 – “Arrangements for the Provision by

Local Authorities of Social Housing through Turnkey Projects”.

• Under this delivery model, local authorities acquire completed residential units

from developers or builders on a turnkey basis. The total acquisition cost

comprises both the site or property acquisition cost and the vouched

construction and development costs, inclusive of VAT, required to deliver the

dwelling to a fully completed turnkey standard.

• In accordance with Circular 31/2019, all project costs are independently

assessed and validated by a Quantity Surveyor appointed by the local

authority. The independent Quantity Surveyor reviews the vouched costs

submitted by the developer and confirms that the costs associated with each

construction element are reasonable and appropriate.

• In addition, turnkey projects are required to comply with the Department’s

prescribed Build Unit Cost (BUC) limits for the relevant local authority area and

dwelling type. These limits vary depending on the size and configuration of the

dwelling (e.g. one-bedroom, two-bedroom and three-bedroom units).

• As turnkey acquisitions involve the delivery of new-build homes, significant

construction and upgrade works are often required to bring properties to a

modern housing standard, including full compliance with current Building

Regulations, particularly the energy efficiency requirements to meet an A BER

rating in energy efficiency. In many cases, properties acquired through this

stream have been vacant, derelict or fire-damaged and require substantial

remediation and reconstruction works before they can be occupied.

• All turnkey funding applications are submitted to the Department of Housing,

Local Government and Heritage for assessment. As part of this process, the

Department’s Technical Unit, including Senior Quantity Surveyors, reviews the

cost plan and project details before funding approval is considered.

• No acquisition can proceed until the Department has completed its

assessment and formally approved the project. Contracts for purchase are only

finalised following receipt of Departmental approval and completion of the

associated legal processes.

**11.Question submitted by Councillor Gavan**

***I will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick what measures are***

***planned to encourage and facilitate cycling along the R445 corridor between Limerick City***

***and Castleconnell/Annacottty/Castletroy. Are there plans for segregated cycling***

***infrastructure/orcas and safer junction treatments on the route.***

**REPLY:** Limerick City and County Council is committed to delivering a safe, segregated cycle

network and enhanced public transport facilities along the R445 arterial corridor, in

line with the Limerick Shannon Metropolitan Area Transport Strategy (LSMATS) and

the NTA's Bus Connects Limerick programme.

Key planned and ongoing completed measures include:

• **BusConnects & Segregated Infrastructure (Dublin Road STC):** The R445 is

designated as a key Sustainable Transport Corridor (STC) under BusConnects.

This major project aims to deliver continuous, dedicated bus lanes running in

tandem with high-quality, fully segregated cycling infrastructure. This is

complemented by the proposed **Limerick City Greenway (UL to Annacotty)**

and the **Castletroy Urban Greenway** schemes.

• **Safer Junction Treatments:** Protected junction designs systematically

integrated into all new schemes. Specifically, major upgrade at the **R445 /**

**R506 (Cappamore Road) junction**, featuring dedicated pedestrian/cyclist

Toucan crossings and segregated cycle tracks to safely manage movements.

• **Castleconnell Greenway Connection:** A UL to Castleconnell & Montpelier

Feasibility and Options Study is currently underway. This study is actively

identifying the safest route to extend the active travel network from Annacotty

out to Castleconnell.

• **Interim Light Segregation:** For sections of the R445 where major

reconstruction is not immediately scheduled, the Active Travel team

continuously evaluates rapid-build measures including light segregation like

bollards or orcas to quickly improve safety on existing lanes, subject to design

and safety audits.

<span data-pagefind-ignore>[[PAGE_END:11]]</span>

The Council and its Active Travel Team continue to work in close partnership

with the National Transport Authority (NTA) to coordinate the design and

delivery of these integrated transport schemes.
