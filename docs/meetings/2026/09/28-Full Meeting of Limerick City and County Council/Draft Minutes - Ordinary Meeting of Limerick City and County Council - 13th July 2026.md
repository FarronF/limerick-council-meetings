Financial Update

**(a)**

**Update on Limerick City and County Council’s** **Financial position**

The Director of Finance, Investment and Governance delivered a presentation outlining the

budget position for 2026. He highlighted a number of financial challenges, including

increasing homelessness service costs, rising fuel costs and the absence of a supplementary

roads allocation to support road maintenance.

The Director of Finance, Investment and Governance advised that a decision will be required

at the September 2026 Council Meeting regarding the variation of the Local Property Tax rate,

noting that the Elected Members may now vary the base rate by up to plus 25% or minus 15%

for the next three years.

Members thanked the Director of Finance, Investment and Governance for his presentation

and raised concerns regarding the increasing costs of homelessness services and fuel.

Members sought clarification on which Government grants had increased by 15% during 2026

and queried whether the Council would be liable for Derelict Property Tax on Council owned

property.

In response to Members’ queries, the Director of Finance, Investment and Governance

confirmed that clarification had not yet been received regarding the new Derelict Property

Tax. He further advised that the Council’s contribution towards the increased cost of

homelessness services amounted to €800,000.

[[PAGE_END:9]]

10

**(b)**

**Update on Mayoral Fund**

The Director of Finance, Investment and Governance delivered an update on the Mayoral

Fund. He advised Members that the total Mayoral Fund allocation for the period 2024 to

2026 amounted to €18.5 million. It was noted that €6 million in unspent capital funding had

been carried forward to 2026, resulting in a total available budget of €14 million for 2026.

The Director of Finance, Investment and Governance advised the Members that funding had

been allocated across a range of current and capital initiatives and noted that it was expected

that the remaining balance would be spent during Q3 and Q4 of 2026.

MAYOR’S QUESTIONS

21.

Question submitted by Councillor E. O’Donovan

***I will ask the Mayor at the next Meeting, in light of the significant disparity in CCTV coverage***

***across the three Metropolitan Districts, with Limerick City West accounting for just 12% of***

***CCTV coverage compared to 34% in Limerick City East and 51% in Limerick City North, and***

***noting that a portion of the 2025 Mayoral Budget was allocated towards the expansion of***

***CCTV infrastructure, will the Mayor outline whether there is a plan to ensure that future***

***CCTV investment is distributed equitably across all Metropolitan Districts, with particular***

***regard to addressing the current shortfall in Limerick City West.***

**REPLY**: It is worth remembering that CCTV across all of Limerick was at serious risk of being

shutdown for 2026 due to lack of funding. The service was saved only through the

intervention of the Mayor and the re-allocation of Mayoral Fund funding for 2026 to

CCTV. However, this is not a sustainable solution and will not be available for 2027.

The Government needs to come up in Budget 2027 with a multi-annual funding source

for this critical service for Limerick. However, despite the Mayor and others raising

this issue at all available opportunities, there has yet been no commitment from

government that this funding will be made available. The Councillor is therefore

encouraged along with others to continue to push government for this issue to be

resolved expeditiously to secure not just these critical services but the jobs which will

otherwise be put at risk in the monitoring station in Moyross.

It is important to note that the Mayoral Fund allocation referred to was not provided

for the expansion of the CCTV network. It was reallocated to extend and maintain the

existing CCTV service during 2026.

The rollout of Community CCTV in Limerick has not historically been planned according

to Metropolitan District or Local Electoral Area boundaries. Rather, the network has

developed over approximately thirty years in response to specific and evidenced

public-safety concerns, particularly the sustained organised crime and gang feuding

experienced in Limerick City from the late 1990s into the 2000s.

During that period, serious and violent crime was heavily concentrated in particular

parts of the city. CCTV systems were consequently installed in the areas where the

level of criminal activity and associated public-safety risk was greatest. Many of those

locations now fall within the Limerick City North and Limerick City East areas.

The systems were installed by Limerick City and County Council in close cooperation

with An Garda Síochána and local communities. Each scheme was authorised by the

Garda Commissioner and continues to play an important role in supporting Garda

investigations. The current distribution of cameras therefore reflects historic, targeted

responses to sustained organised criminal activity rather than a recent policy of

allocating CCTV infrastructure by geographical area.

[[PAGE_END:15]]

16

Future proposals for CCTV installations must likewise be considered primarily on the

basis of identified risks, patterns of criminal behaviour and demonstrated public-

safety need, rather than geographical location alone. Any proposal must:

• satisfy the requirements of necessity, justification and proportionality under Part 5,

section 28 of the Garda Síochána (Recording Devices) Act 2023;

• have the support of the relevant community, local councillors and An Garda

Síochána;

and

• have sufficient funding for both the installation of the system and its ongoing

operation and maintenance.

The application and approval process is resource-intensive and can take a

considerable period to complete, with final approval required from the Garda

Commissioner.

A recent example in Limerick City West is the new CCTV scheme for the People’s Park.

This proposal was developed in response to clear, evidence-based public-safety

concerns, including consistent reports of drug dealing and violent incidents in and

around the area. The application took approximately ten months to prepare, was

submitted in October 2024 and received the approval of the Garda Commissioner in

February 2026.

While there is a clear disparity in the present number of CCTV cameras across the

metropolitan areas, this did not result from a planned or inequitable allocation of

CCTV infrastructure based on geographical boundaries. It arose from the targeted

development of the network over time in response to the locations experiencing the

highest levels of serious and organised crime.

Any future investment should continue to be directed towards proposals supported

by objective evidence of public-safety need and developed in cooperation with

communities, elected members and An Garda Síochána. However, the capacity to

maintain the existing service or develop additional schemes—including schemes in

Limerick City West—will depend on the availability of sustainable, multi-annual

Government funding.

Councillor O’Donovan noted that the CCTV network in Limerick had developed since its

**REPLY**: Housing Maintenance

The Council's policy is to return vacant properties to a lettable standard prior to

allocation. While the objective of minimising vacancy periods and accelerating

allocations is fully supported, there are several practical considerations that must be

addressed before a property can be occupied.

These include:

•

**Health & Safety Requirements:** The Council has a duty of care to ensure that all

properties are safe for occupation. Essential works may be required to address

electrical, gas, fire safety, structural, asbestos, dampness, mould, or other health

and safety-related issues before a tenancy can commence.

•

**Access to the Property:** Once a property is allocated and occupied, access for

Council staff and contractors can become more restricted. Completing works prior

to occupation generally allows for safer, more efficient and less disruptive delivery

of refurbishment and upgrade works.

•

**Contractor and Resource Availability:** The scheduling of works is dependent on

the availability of direct labour crews, specialist contractors and materials. Some

works must be completed in a specific sequence and cannot be readily undertaken

with tenants in residence.

•

**Tenant Welfare and Disruption:** Carrying out refurbishment works in occupied

homes can create inconvenience, noise, dust, and potential safety risks for

tenants, particularly where vulnerable households, children, older persons, or

people with disabilities are involved.

Notwithstanding these considerations, the Council continually reviews its void

management processes to identify opportunities for the earlier allocation of

properties where it is safe, practical, and in the best interests of both the incoming

tenant and the Council.

Housing Allocations

The Council works closely to ensure suitable applicants are identified for properties

before refurbishment works are completed, thereby minimising vacancy periods. The

[[PAGE_END:25]]

26

introduction of Choice Based Letting later this year, on a pilot basis, will further reduce

turnaround times, particularly in harder-to-let areas of Limerick City and County.

The proposal is worthy of further consideration, and the Council will examine whether

such an approach could be feasible and, if so, how it might operate in practice. This

review will need to consider the legal, health and safety, operational, resource and

tenant welfare implications involved. The outcome of that examination may

determine that the proposed approach is not possible to operationalise; however, the

Council is committed to giving the matter proper consideration before reaching a
