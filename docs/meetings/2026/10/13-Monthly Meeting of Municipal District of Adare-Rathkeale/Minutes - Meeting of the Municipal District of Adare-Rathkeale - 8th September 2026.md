**Question submitted by Councillor B. Collins**

**I will ask at the next Meeting that Limerick City and County Council outline the**

**procedure, backed by legislation and regulations, on the changing of a property, which**

**was previously in commercial use, to private residential, setting out all the necessary**

**applications, notifications, sections and other procedures that must be in place be**

**compliant with national and local policy.**

[[PAGE_END:6]]

7

**Reply:**

The change of use of a property from commercial to residential

constitutes development under the Planning and Development Act, 2000

(as amended) and as such whereby planning permission may be required.

The procedures for same are detailed in the relevant statue.

The Planning and Development Regulations 2001 (as amended) set out

exempted development provisions for certain changes of uses; however,

these exemptions may depend on a range of land use criteria, including

but not limited to, for example if the property is a protected structure, its

location and the planning history of the site.

Should a member of the public wish to receive confirmation as to whether

planning permission is required, a request can be made to the Planning

Authority under Section 5 of the Planning and Development Act, 2000 (as

amended) for a determination.

The Planning Department would be happy to liaise with Cllr. Collins on the

options available on a site-specific basis.

duplication and commitments previously given to projects.

Elected Members during the adoption of the Development Contribution

scheme, the Forward Planning Section met with the Cathaoirleach of each

Municipal and Metro District to discuss projects in the context of local

General. From there, the funding was transferred to the relevant Limerick

City and County Council sections.

projects and funding requirements for 2027.

**16.**

**Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor J. O’Donoghue**

**I will move at the next Meeting that Housing Developments with fully paid Planning**

**contributions to Limerick City and County Council in the last ten years have their**

**development contributions considered when applying as part of a Local Improvement**

**or Community Involvement Scheme.**

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor O’Donoghue, seconded by Councillor

Teskey and agreed.

In proposing the Motion Councillor O’Donoghue amended the wording slight to read:

“I will move at the next Meeting that Housing Developments with fully paid Planning

contributions to Limerick City and County Council in the *previous* ten years have their

development contributions considered when applying as part of a Local Improvement or

Community Involvement Scheme”.

Councillor O’Donoghue felt people who purchased a site in a rural area and already pay

a development contribution for the upkeep of the roads in its vicinity should be entitled

to have this factor considered due to the fact they have already contributed towards the

upkeep of the road during the planning stages of their property.

**Reply:**

Development contributions are levied under Section 48 of the Planning

and Development Act 2000 and are intended to fund public infrastructure

and facilities that support development including roads, transportation,

drainage, community infrastructure and related capital projects. Limerick

City and County Council applies these charges through its Development

Contribution Scheme.

The Local Improvement Scheme (LIS) is a national programme funded by

the Department of Rural and Community Development to improve certain

private or non-public roads, primarily those providing access to

agricultural holdings and rural land users. Local Authorities administer and

prioritise applications in accordance with the Department guidelines. The

[[PAGE_END:11]]

12

beneficiaries are normally required to contribute to the cost normally up

to 10% of the overall costs of the works.

Community Involvement schemes (CIS) is a programme operated by

Limerick City and County Council to facilitate the improvement of public

roads and local infrastructure where there is significant community

benefit and where local residents are willing to contribute financially

towards the works. The local community contributes to the cost of the

scheme generally up to 10% to the overall cost.

Development fund contributions are charged

• As a condition of planning

• To offset infrastructure costs associated with new development.

• On a county wide or area wide basis

LIS and CIS programmes are intended to address specific local

infrastructure deficiencies and are funded through separate mechanisms.

Furthermore, the grants available to the applicants are generous with the

Department generally covering 90% of the overall cost of each scheme.

The payment of the Development charge does not create an entitlement

to preference under another scheme. Furthermore, LIS and CIS schemes

are governed by Department eligibility criteria. Local Authorities must

prioritise applications within National guidelines. Any preferential

treatment based on development contribution payment would conflict

with those criteria.
