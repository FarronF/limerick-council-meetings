**4.Limerick City Centre**

**Update on Halloween in Limerick 2026**

Members received a presentation from the Administrative Officer, Economic and Tourism

Development, on the proposed Halloween in Limerick 2026 festival programme. The

presentation outlined a city and county-wide festival taking place from 11th October to 1st

November 2026, supported by Fáilte Ireland's Home of Halloween initiative, with a focus on

celebrating the traditions of Samhain through culture, heritage, storytelling, community

participation and tourism experiences.

The presentation highlighted a number of flagship events, including the Mystery Samhain

Banquet, Trick or Treat in the City, the Halloween City Centre Fancy Dress 5km, Oktoberfeis,

the Limerick Harvest Festival, and the Rathkeale Halloween Festival – “Dullahan to Darkness”,

together with a range of additional cultural and community events across the city and county.

Members were advised that the programme aims to increase visitor numbers, support local

businesses and the hospitality sector, activate both urban and rural locations, and further

strengthen Limerick's position as a leading Halloween destination. The anticipated economic,

tourism and community benefits of the festival were also outlined.

Following a discussion, and in response to Members’ queries regarding the absence of a

parade this year, the Head of Economic and Tourism Development advised that a tender

process had been undertaken, with Lumen Street Theatre being the only organisation to

submit a tender. Following an independent assessment, funding of €40,000 was allocated for

the parade; however, this was declined by Lumen Street Theatre, as it considered the funding

offered to be insufficient.

Members were further advised that as no other submissions were received through the

tender process, the funding was reallocated to other events.

**21.Question submitted by Councillor O’Donovan**

***I will ask at the next Meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick for an update on the***

***Irish Cement Community Liaison Committee, including the total amount of funding collected***

***to date through the Community Gain Fund, and for an update on the position regarding the***

***provision for three elected representatives to sit on the Community Liaison Committee.***

**REPLY:** On April 12, 2018, An Bord Pleanala granted permission to Irish Cement,

Castlemungret to develop land to facilitate on site handling, storage and introduction

of alternative fuels with conveyor kiln, storage tanks, uploading station, handling

building, cooling tower and associated ancillary works. This permission was subject to

thirteen conditions. Condition 11 of that order stated “A Community Liaison

Committee shall be established to liaise between the operator of the cement works,

the planning authority and the local community. The committee shall comprise seven

members having an independent chairperson, two local community representatives,

two elected members of the planning authority, one official of the planning authority

and one representative from the operator of the cement works. The Community

Liaison Committee shall have responsibility for the administration of the community

gain fund account to be set up in accordance with condition number 12 of this order

and for decisions on projects to be supported by the find in addition to acting as a

liaison committee with the local community in relation to ongoing monitoring of the

operation of the cement works.”

At the November 2025 plenary meeting of Limerick City and County Council, a request

was made for the mandated number of elected members(2) to be appointed to the

CLC. As more than two members put their names forward, it was agreed that

correspondence would be sent to An Bord Pleanala seeking permission to add one

additional elected member to the committee, bringing the complement on the

committee to three elected representatives. Our colleagues in Planning have written

to An Bord Pleanala for guidance on the possibility of altering the original ruling,

however, to date, no response has been received.

The original ruling from An Bord Pleanala allowed for 90,000 tonnes of alternative

materials to be used in the manufacturing process and set a rate of one euro per tonne

to be set aside for the community gain fund. To date, there has been one payment of

<span data-pagefind-ignore>[[PAGE_END:12]]</span>

50% of the total fund i.e. €45,000. This sum was received by Limerick City and County

Council on May 11th, 2026. The schedule of drawdown of the balance of the fund will

be a matter for the Community Liaison Committee members to decide.

It is important that the Community Liaison Committee be convened as soon as

possible, and while an outcome from the request to An Bord Pleanala is awaited, the

Council remains available to assist the members in selecting two committee members

with the possibility of a third elected member being added should the response from

An Bord Pleanala indicate that it is in order to do so.

on behalf of homeless clients are expedited by the Assessments Team.

A designated member of the Assessments Team deals exclusively with homeless

applications and liaises directly with the Homeless Action Team where any additional

or missing information is required.

Where an application is submitted directly to the Assessments Team by an applicant

who states that they are homeless or couch surfing, the Assessments Team will liaise

with the Homeless Action Team to establish whether the applicant has been assessed

by their unit and, if so, the outcome of that assessment.

If the applicant has not previously contacted the Homeless Action Team and is advised

by the Assessments Team to do so, confirmation from the Homeless Action Team that

the applicant has been assessed by them and is eligible to use the Homeless Action

Team address for correspondence is required before this can be accepted as their

address for the housing application.

In the absence of such confirmation, a certified address is required. Where an

applicant does not have a certified tenancy, for example, where they are couch

surfing, the following documentation is required:

• A letter from the householder confirming that the applicant is residing at the

address;

• A utility bill or other official bill in the householder’s name for that address; and

• An official document in the applicant’s own name showing that same address. This

may include a bank statement, social welfare statement, Revenue document, or

similar official correspondence.

Every effort is made by the Assessments Team to assist applicants and facilitate the

process as much as possible, particularly where there are difficulties in providing the

required documentation.

**27.Question submitted by Councillor Donoghue**

***I will ask at the next Meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick what services are in***

***place to assist families living in private rented accommodation, who do not qualify for social***

***housing, and who are facing eviction.***

**REPLY:** Households facing eviction are encouraged to engage with the Homeless Action Team

(HAT) at their earliest opportunity. The team will assess their individual circumstances

and provide information on available supports and accommodation options that may

require placement into emergency accommodation. Where appropriate, the

Homeless Action Team will signpost or refer households to relevant services and

supports such as tenancy protection services available through Threshold. Threshold

provides support and guidance on illegal evictions, unreasonable rent increases and

tenancy terminations. The Homeless Action Team may also refer families to the

Residential Tenancies Board (RTB) if they are having landlord disputes. In some cases,

households may also be assessed under the Cost Rental Tenant In-Situ (CRTiS)

Scheme, but this is subject to income thresholds and landlord intention to sell the

property.

<span data-pagefind-ignore>[[PAGE_END:20]]</span>

Persons living in private rented accommodation can make an application for a Local

Authority Home Loan to purchase a property in the Limerick City & County Council

area of operation. The applicant(s) must fulfil the scheme eligibility requirements as

well as undergoing a credit check. Where a person has been issued with a valid Notice

to Quit (NTQ) and meets the loan eligibility criteria, a decision on their application can

be fast tracked. If this is successful, they are issued with an Approval in Principle, valid

for 6 months. Limerick City & County Council do not assist in sourcing properties for

them.

**29.Question submitted by Councillor Donoghue**

***I will ask at the next Meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick if a CCTV application***

***can be commenced for the Baggot Estate community garden following a number of***

***malicious fires and vandalism that took place there over the Summer.***

**REPLY:** Limerick City and County Council can commence the preliminary assessment process

for a potential Community CCTV scheme at the Baggot Estate Community Garden.

However, it should be noted that Community CCTV schemes are subject to a lengthy

and complex statutory approval process and cannot proceed unless it can be

demonstrated that CCTV is justified, necessary and proportionate in response to an

identified crime or public safety issue. The early stages of the process involve

consultation with the Council's Data Protection Officer and Digital Services

Department and it is heavily reliant on a Garda Analyst Crime Report, which examines

crime and incident statistics for the relevant area over the preceding five years and

forms a key part of the evidence required to assess whether a CCTV scheme can be

justified.

Should the Garda analysis support the proposal, a detailed CCTV project proposal

would then be required, which includes the identification of a sustainable funding

source. It should be strongly noted that the availability of funding is a critical factor in

determining whether any Community CCTV scheme can progress. In addition to the

significant capital costs associated with installation, provision must also be made for

ongoing costs of maintenance, repairs, connectivity and lifecycle replacement of

equipment. At present, there is no identified funding or budget available to fund the

development of any new Community CCTV schemes.

<span data-pagefind-ignore>[[PAGE_END:21]]</span>

Consequently, while the initial assessment process can commence, progression

beyond the early stages would be dependent on the outcome of the Garda analysis

and the identification of an appropriate funding source.

Ballyhoura Development, West Limerick Resources, Paul Partnership, Limerick PPN,

can regulate, exercise, and engage in meaningful recreation in a way that supports

**35.Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor Secas**

***I will move at the next Meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick that this District calls***

***on the Council to establish a dedicated supplementary development contribution scheme***

***for the wider Castletroy area, with all development contribution levies generated within the***

***area ringfenced and reinvested exclusively in infrastructure, community facilities, and other***

***strategic projects required to support the area's significant growth.***

In proposing the Motion Councillor Secas outlined the need for basic infrastructure in the

Castletroy area. The Motion was seconded by Councillor Doyle.

Members expressed concerns that establishing a dedicated supplementary development

contribution scheme for the Castletroy area could create disparities between different parts

of the Metropolitan District and have a negative impact on investment in other areas.

<span data-pagefind-ignore>[[PAGE_END:27]]</span>

The Senior Planner advised that, under the provisions of the Planning and Development Act

2000, as amended, and the current Development Contribution Scheme, this could not be

facilitated.

Some Members indicated their opposition to the Motion suggesting that ringfencing

development contributions for a specific area would be contrary to the principle of equitable

distribution of resources and could disadvantage less affluent communities.

Members requested a workshop on existing and potential funding opportunities for

infrastructure and community development projects. The Acting Director agreed to facilitate

the workshop.

**REPLY:** The Planning and Development Act 2000, as amended, provides for general

development contributions under Section 48, supplementary development

contributions under Section 49 and special development contributions under Section

48(2)(c).

The Council’s Development Contribution Scheme 2025–2031, adopted under Section

48, is based on an indicative €209 million capital programme and already includes

projects serving Castletroy and the wider area. These include the M7 Junction 28

Mackey Junction and the National Technology Park/University of Limerick Link Road,

the Groody Road slip road and bus lane, Groody Valley Park, a new Castletroy library

and graveyard, community centre infrastructure and pre-development works.

Section 49 permits a supplementary scheme only for a defined area and a specified

public infrastructure project or service that directly benefits the development

charged. Any supplementary levy would be additional to the general Section 48

contribution.

The 2013 *Development Contributions Guidelines for Planning Authorities* expressly

caution against double charging. It would therefore be inappropriate to impose a

Section 49 contribution for infrastructure already included in the capital programme

underpinning the adopted Section 48 Scheme as set out above.

Accordingly, having regard to the recent adoption of the Section 48 Scheme, the

inclusion of significant Castletroy projects and the requirement to avoid double

charging, the infrastructure requirements of the wider Castletroy area are more

appropriately addressed, at present, through the implementation and ongoing review

of the adopted Section 48 Scheme

The establishment of a dedicated Supplementary Development Contribution Scheme

for the wider Castletroy area would require the identification and costings of specific

infrastructure projects, that are not already included in the Section 48 scheme, and

the definition of the geographic area to which the scheme would apply.
