4. NVIRONMENT, CLI A N & Fl

The Cathaoirleach welcomed the Senior Executive Scientist to the Meeting. He proceeded to
give a PowerPoint presentation to the Members on issues related to treating invasive alien
species in Co. Limerick. He explained that an Invasive Alien Species (IAS) Is defined as a
terrestrial or aquatic plant, animal, insect, or microorganism that has colonised and invaded
a new country or environment and causes substantial environmental and economic
damage.

He confirmed that landowners and occupiers under Irish legislation are responsible for
dealing with and preventing the spread of invasive species on their own properties, whilst
Limerick City & County Council is responsible for the lands it owns or manages.

The effective control methods for treating same included:

* Foliar Spraying: Spray a 5% to 8% giyphosate-based weedkiller onto fully developed
leaves in early summer, followed by a second application in the Autumn;

* Stem Injection: Inject undiluted glyphosate directly into the hollow chambers of
mature stems using an industrial syringe during late summer;

* Professional Excavation: Dig out the entire root and rhizome system completely for
construction sites, though this requires licensed disposal.

He also confirmed that it was important that landowners do not cut or strim invasive species
plants as trimming the stems causes tiny root fragments to resprout and worsen the
infestation. It was also important that material was not composted as plant waste remains
viable and can spread if added to home compost heaps.

The Members thanked the Senior Executive Scientist for his presentation, They agreed that
the issue of invasive species was a major problem for not only local authority lands, but also
for individual landowners who were trying to deal with it on their own land, as it was very
costly and required to be treated for a period of 5 years before being fully eradicated from
land.

They noted that incidences of invasive species could be logged and mapped by the Council
and they agreed that the public should be made more aware of the issue as it was important
for the public to continue to report it so that the issue could be tracked and managed as
best as possible.

8. Question submitted by Councillor Carey

| will ask at the next Meeting of the Cappamore-Kilmallock Municipal District for an update
on the two preliminary surveys suggested regarding my Motion from January 2026 on
proposed walkway lighting in Kilmallock.

Reply:

In response to the Motion adopted in January 2026 regarding the provision of public lighting
along walkways in Kilmallock, preliminary ecological assessments have now been
completed, including both daytime and nighttime site surveys undertaken on 9" September
2026. The surveys were carried out to evaluate the potential impact of additional lighting on
local wildlife and habitats.

Four walkways were assessed: the Priory Walk; Memorial Walk; Loobagh Walkway and West
Wall Walkway. The findings confirmed significant bat activity along the Priory Walk,
Memorial Walk and Loobagh Walkway, with four bat species recorded in total. The surveys
also identified the presence of Grey Heron along the Priory Walk. Given the ecological
importance of the River Loobagh as a wildlife corridor and the potential for additional
lighting to adversely affect nocturnal wildlife, it has been concluded that these three routes
are not suitable for the provision of public lighting.

The West Wall Walkway was identified as the most suitable location for lighting due to the
limited amount of bat activity recorded and the existing level of surrounding illumination.
The assessment recommends that any lighting proposed for this route should be in the form
of low-level, wildlife-friendly bollard lighting designed to minimise light spill and avoid
impacts on local biodiversity.

| will ask at the next Meeting of the Cappamore-Kilmallock Municipal District if Cappamore
Development Association would be allowed to light up Cappamore Library for Christmas
2026.

Reply:

Cappamore Library and Artists Studio play a central role in developing community
participation and cohesion in Cappamore. The Library would be delighted in facilitating
Cappamore Development Association with the lighting up of Cappamore Library for
Christmas.

It is worth noting that the Library is required under Health & Safety legislation to ensure that
all temporary electrical installations are carried out by a qualified electrician, currently
qualified with Safe Electric. The electrician will be required to provide documentary
certification to Cappamore Library that the temporary work has been carried out to the
required current safety standards and technical rules.

The Library would be happy to provide the electrician selected by Cappamore Development
Association with a sample copy of the certification required in advance of the works.
