Local Community Safety Partnership

The Meetings Administrator stated that Limerick City and County Council was required to

consider the nomination of seven Members to the Limerick City and County Local Community

Safety Partnership

Proposed by Councillor Collins (M);

Seconded by Councillor Teefy;

“That, Councillors Benson, Collins (B), Conway, Kiely, Reale, Scanlan and Slattery, be

appointed to the Limerick City and County Local Community Safety Partnership.”

As there were no other nominations, the Príomh Chomhairleoir declared Councillors Benson,

Collins (B), Conway, Kiely, Reale, Scanlan and Slattery appointed to the Limerick City and

County Local Community Safety Partnership.

Members expressed disappointment in relation to the number of Councillors allowed to sit

on the Local Community Safety Partnership Committee (LCSP) in Limerick, noting that

representation of seven out of forty-one Elected Members is disproportionate. They

suggested that consideration be given to the establishing sub-committees.

The Members were informed that as part of the conditions of the Community Safety

Partnership, seven Elected Members were to be appointed to the Committee. It was noted

that, once the full committee was established, the LCSP could consider sub-committee

structures.

Question submitted by Councillor E. O’Donovan

***I will ask at the next Meeting of Limerick City and County Council for the number of animal***

***welfare complaints received last year and the current resources available to the local***

***authority for enforcement of the Animal Health and Welfare Act (2013).***

**REPLY:** A review of the Council’s records shows that 27 animal welfare or cruelty cases were

reported in 2025.

The Council does not have an Authorised Officer appointed under the Animal Health

and Welfare Act. As a result, we do not have the authority to enforce the provisions

of the Act. Responsibility for implementing the Act lies primarily with the Department

of Agriculture, Food and the Marine (DAFM). All animal welfare cases received by the

Council are therefore referred to DAFM. The Council’s capacity to address such

matters has been further reduced following the transfer of the Local Authority

Veterinary Officer post to DAFM.

Council staff continue to operate under the Control of Dogs Act, the Control of Horses

Act, and the Dog Breeding Establishments Act, and will take action on cases that fall

within the scope of these legislative provisions.

Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor E. Ryan

***I will move at the next Meeting that Limerick City and County Council provide independent***

***legal advice to all its Elected Members on whether or not they have a conflict of interest***

***when called as a witness on behalf of a constituent when the case is being taken by Limerick***

***City and County Council.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Ryan (E) and seconded by Councillor

Scanlan.

In proposing the Motion, Councillor Ryan (E) outlined the background to the case. Members,

in supporting the Motion, emphasised the importance of independent legal advice for the

Elected Members.

The Director General advised that, in the event of any Member being called as a witness,

Corporate Services could work with the Member to seek appropriate advice.

**REPLY**: With regard to the issue of conflict of interest, the advice is that unless the Elected

Members have been involved in the decision made to prosecute it is unlikely that a

Member could have a conflict of interest. The decision to prosecute is an executive

function; Elected Members do not have a role in this executive function.

If in any case where the Council has initiated proceedings, and an Elected Member is

called by the subject of the proceedings, the Council will arrange for separate legal

service advice to be provided if an Elected Member believes they might have a conflict

of interest.

Notice of Motion submitted by John Moran, Mayor of Limerick

***I will move at the next Meeting that this Council agrees, subject to the necessary***

***administrative, legal, procurement and data protection arrangements being put in place,***

***and with financial support secured so as to protect existing core services in 2026, that all***

***meetings of the Full Council held after the adoption of this resolution shall be live-streamed***

***and recorded in full by audio and video means, and that such recordings shall be made***

***publicly available on the Council’s official website (**www.limerick.ie**) no later than two***

***business days following the conclusion of each meeting, without unnecessary barriers or***

***registration requirements.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Mayor Moran and seconded by Councillor Hartigan

(S).

In proposing the Motion, the Mayor highlighted the importance of making Meetings

accessible to the public and noted that this may require additional technological supports. He

advised that Councillor O’Donovan had agreed to contribute GMA funding towards the costs.

Members discussed the value of live-streaming and recording Meetings to enhance public

understanding on how local democracy operates. Concerns were expressed in relation to

associated costs, as well as legal and data protection implications.

Councillor Collins (M) proposed an amendment to the Motion to include “all Statutory

Meetings”. This was seconded by Councillor Collins (B).

The Mayor advised that the Mayoral Fund would cover the cost of implementing the

recording of Full Council Meetings and noted that Standing Orders could be reviewed to

incorporate this provision; any expansion to other Meetings would require further

consideration.

**REPLY**:

This response considers the implications of live streaming and digitally recording, to make

publicly available, the proceedings and recordings of the Plenary Meetings of Limerick City

and County Council.

The Members will be aware that under Part 6 of the Local Government Act 2001 (as amended)

members of the public are entitled to be present at a meeting of the local authority. In

Limerick this has been facilitated through the public and press galleries for in person

attendance and, since the introduction of hybrid meetings, online for members of the public

and press.

Standing Orders

The livestreaming of Council and broadcasting of recordings is not provided for under the

Standing Orders.

The full Standing Orders are currently under review by the Standing Orders Sub Committee.

Any amendment requires that at least one half of the total number of Members of the Council

vote in favour of amending.

Technical Capacity and Software

The current system in use was commissioned for internal use and is not appropriate for roll

out to meet requirements of data protection, freedom of information, storage and broadcast

capability.

While livestreaming can occur at a cost of €10,000 & vat per annum with additional staff

resources, ICT and the Data Protection Unit have recommended that a managed solution is

required where the recording and publication process is automated and supported.

Consultation local authorities who currently record meetings confirms the use of a managed

solution. Initial marketing soundings the estimated costs of suitable software with built-in

governance and technical capabilities to allow for use in the Council Chamber is

approximately €30,000 p.a. & vat subject to proper procurement.

**Conclusion**

While live streaming and recording public meetings can provide transparency and accessibility

benefits, it also presents legal, data privacy, financial and reputational risks. It is noted that

the Members of Council have qualified legal privilege, not absolute parliamentary privilege.

It is recommended that if the decision is to proceed it is appropriately resourced in order to

ensure an effective solution.
