**4.Limerick City Centre**

**(a)Limerick City Retail Update**

Members received an update from the Administrative Officer, Economic and Tourism

Development, on City and Town Centre Retail Development initiatives in Limerick. It was

noted that Bannon Consultants were appointed in February 2026 to prepare a City Centre

Retail Strategy and Visions and Options Report (2026–2040). Analysis of the current retail

landscape has been completed, with stakeholder engagement ongoing. The Administrative

Officer, Economic and Tourism Development advised that a draft report is expected by

mid-May 2026 and that further presentations will be arranged for the Members.

Members were also updated on the following initiatives:

• The Limerick Gift Card

• The Vacant Business Premises Rates Incentive Scheme 2026

• The High Street Innovators Competition 2026.

It was further noted that, despite challenging economic conditions, 21 new businesses

opened in Limerick City in 2025.

Members thanked the team for the presentation and highlighted initiatives like the Limerick

Gift Card as a positive and welcome support for the retail sector. Members acknowledged

the ongoing pressures facing retailers, including challenges associated with opening new

businesses. Members requested early engagement with Councillors on matters such as

strategy development, and it was agreed that a workshop would be arranged to discuss the

Retail Strategy.

**(b)City Centre Response Team Update**

An overview of the 2026 works programme for the city centre was provided by the Executive

Engineer, City Centre Response Team, the Licensing Enforcement Officer, City Centre

Response Team and the Executive Engineer, Cleansing Team, which included updates on

footpath refurbishments, laneway improvements, pedestrianisation works, installation of
---
bollards and bin storage units, seating upgrades, lighting pole refurbishment, park and

garden enhancements, drainage works, and refurbishment projects at key locations such as

Shannon Street, Arthur’s Quay, Irishtown, the Hunt Museum Gardens, O’Connell Street

Fountain and the Tait Clock.

Members were advised that waste management, trip and fall hazards, and general

maintenance remain ongoing challenges in the city centre. The team responds to issues on a

continual basis, with examples provided of hazards being identified and rectified promptly.

The Licensing Enforcement Officer gave an outline of his role to the Members, including

management of casual trading markets, processing of event trading and Section 254 street

furniture licences and inspections, to prevent unauthorised trading.

The Executive Engineer, Cleansing, highlighted the work of the city centre cleansing service,

which operates seven days a week, with 40 staff assigned. Works include bin cleaning, deep

cleansing, graffiti removal, waste removal, weekend clean-ups, vacuum street cleaning and

power washing across the city centre.

Members thanked the team for the presentation, highlighting the value of their work in the

city centre and commended the excellent response to customer relationship management

(CRM) requests. Members noted the former lunchtime food market at Honan’s Quay and

queried if this initiative could be re‑established. The team advised that they would engage

with traders and explore the matter further.

**(c)Riverfest Limerick Update**

The Head of Economic and Tourism Development presented Members with an overview of

the Riverfest Limerick 2026 programme, scheduled to take place from 1–4 May 2026 over

the May Bank Holiday weekend. Riverfest was outlined as Limerick’s flagship summer

festival, positioning the city as a destination for families, food, culture and sport.

The Head of Economic and Tourism Development gave up an update on the key elements of

the core programme for 2026, including the Riverfestival Village at Arthur’s Quay Park,

Fashionfest, Riverfest Wellness, street parties, and the Riverfest Spectacular Fireworks

display. A further update was provided on Night-Time Economy initiatives, including Twilight

Thursday, live performances, cultural events and city-wide entertainment throughout the

weekend.

Members were informed that there is a strong emphasis on family-friendly and inclusive

programming for Riverfest 2026, with events taking place across the city, including the Milk

Market, Hunt Museum, city streets and riverfront locations. The Great Limerick Run – Run

for Fun, for which Limerick City and County Council is title sponsor, also forms part of the

programme.

Members thanked the team for the presentation and commended them for their work.

Members referred to the Riverfest Sustainability Report 2025 and queried why it hasn’t been

circulated to date. Some Members emphasised the importance of agreeing a theme for
---
Limerick Riverfest and requested that a festival sub‑group would be established, similar to

the Christmas sub-group.

In response to the Members’ requests, the Head of Economic and Tourism Development

advised that workshops and/or the establishment of a sub-group for Riverfest 2027 would

be considered and confirmed that the Riverfest Sustainability Report would be circulated to

Members after the Meeting.

**7.Regeneration, Community, Sport and Rural Development Directorate**

**(a)Dooradoyle/Raheen/Mungret Community Centre Update**

The A/Senior Executive Officer, Community gave a presentation to the Members outlining

the background, need and proposed next steps for a multi-purpose community centre

serving the Dooradoyle/Raheen area, informed by the Mayoral Plan, LECP and a feasibility

study completed in 2022.

The study noted that the demographic overview highlighted significant population growth in

the areas and outlined that community infrastructure was required to support sustainable

growth. It further recommended a two-community model, recognising distinct needs in

Dooradoyle/Raheen and Mungret, and concluded that a separate community centre is

required to address the current deficit in the Dooradoyle/Raheen area while avoiding

duplication of facilities.

Proposed next steps include site selection, development of a modern, flexible and inclusive

design, establishment of a community-led governance model, and identification of potential

funding streams. Indicative costs and a phased implementation roadmap from 2026 to 2030

were outlined, subject to site availability, funding and partnerships.

Members thanked the team for their presentation and work to date. The Cathaoirleach

acknowledged and credited the Councillors for their role in providing GMA funding to

support the feasibility study for the area. Some Members expressed their disappointment at

the length of time it is taking to deliver a multi-purpose community centre for the area, and

other Members highlighted the ongoing need for a community centre in other areas such as

Castletroy and Garryowen.

***Flood Defences project will include any design proposals to demarcate to the public the***

**REPLY:** We can confirm that the medieval wall will indeed be demarcated within the

clearly communicated in the final design.

**11.Question submitted by Councillor Doyle**

***I will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick is there any grant or***

***incentive scheme to encourage retail shop owners to improve their shop front signage.***

***Some shop fronts/signage on William Street are of poor quality and not complying with***

***regulations.***

**REPLY:** Historic shopfronts to Protected Structures and/or those located within Architectural

Conservation Areas (ACAs) are eligible for grant support for conservation and repair

works under the Built Heritage Investment Scheme. While William Street is not

within an Architectural Conservation Area, it does contain a number of Protected

Structures.

The Built Heritage Investment Scheme is closed for 2026. Dates for the 2027 scheme

have yet to be confirmed; however, the application process is expected to launch in

July 2026, with a likely application deadline in September.

Details of the grant will be circulated to all staff and Councillors upon launch,

published on Limerick.ie, and promoted through Limerick City and County Council’s

social media channels. In addition, the Conservation Section maintains a list of

interested contacts who will be notified directly when the scheme opens.

Other than the Built Heritage Investment Scheme outlined above, there is no grant or

incentive scheme to improve shopfront signage in the Metropolitan area.

***I will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick for an update on***

***followed by the Park Rangers in advance of closing times, and an update on the locations***

***for opening and closing times in these parks.***

**REPLY:** Both the People’s Park and Baggott Estate are open for 8am every morning year

round. However, the closing time schedule is seasonal to reflect daylight hours. Since

the beginning of April, the parks are open until 8pm every evening and this will be

extended to 9pm from May to August 2026.

In addition to the Park closing times being displayed at the entrances to each Park, in

advance of closing the gates at the people’s Park, the Park Ranger rings the bell 20

minutes before the Park is due to close around the perimeter and within the Park to

**34.Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor Beasley**

***I will move at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick that Limerick City***

***and County Council, in conjunction with An Garda Siochana and the management of TUS***

***Gaelic Grounds, urgently review and strengthen the traffic and parking management plan***

***for all major GAA fixtures, in light of the severe and recurring disruption experienced by***

***residents of Caherdavin Lawn and the surrounding estates.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Beasley, seconded by Councillor Hickey-

O’Mara and agreed.

In proposing the Motion, Councillor Beasley stressed the importance of implementing

enforcement measures to protect residents from ongoing disruption.

**REPLY:** Traffic and parking management arrangements for major fixtures at the TUS Gaelic

Grounds are managed through established coordination and planning processes.

Ahead of large attendance events, the GAA convenes pre-event meetings with all

relevant statutory agencies, including An Garda Síochána and Limerick City and

County Council, this includes discussion on operational and traffic arrangements.

Parking for stadium staff, stewards and wheelchair users is provided within the

stadium grounds and at designated nearby locations, including Dunnes Stores.

Parking is available in city-centre car parks with additional match-day parking for

patrons facilitated by Na Piarsaigh GAA.
---
An Garda Síochána, as the Traffic Authority, is the body responsible for planning,

implementing and enforcing the Traffic Management Plan, including road closures,

diversions, parking controls, and post-match traffic flows.

These arrangements are communicated to the public in advance through media and

other channels.

An Garda Síochána prepare a comprehensive Garda Traffic Management Plan for a

capacity crowds of approximately 44,000 spectators. This includes widespread

temporary road closures and diversions on the Ennis Road, Shelbourne Road, Cratloe

Road and surrounding areas, restrictions on access to residential estates, and strong

enforcement against illegal parking and obstructions, including ticketing and towing

where necessary. Patrons are encouraged to use city-centre parking facilities and

walk to the stadium.

Post-match traffic management measures prioritise the safe and efficient dispersal of

patrons, with controlled outbound routes, temporary contra-flow traffic systems, and

managed inbound access to the city. Additional measures set out arrangements for

coach parking, disability access, prohibited items such as flares and smoke bombs,

intoxication controls and general crime-prevention advice for attendees.

The Garda traffic and parking management measures in place for major fixtures at

the TUS Gaelic Grounds are reviewed for each event, with a strong emphasis on

advance planning, public communication, enforcement, and the protection of

residential areas.
