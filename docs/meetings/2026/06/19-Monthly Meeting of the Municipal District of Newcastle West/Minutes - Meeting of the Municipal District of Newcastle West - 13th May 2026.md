Ss. Question submitted by Councillor L. Galvin

! will ask at the next Meeting can the Council open inlets from a public road to a farmers
land where there is no dyke to carry the surface water.

REPLY:

Yes, the local authority can open inlets from a public road to private lands under Section 76
of the Roads Act 1993.

There is an onus on the Road Authority to manage and maintain road drainage to prevent
surface water ponding on public roads. Surface water ponding on the carriageway poses a
significant safety risk to road users and also leads to the deterioration of the road surface.
Under Section 76(1) of the Roads Act 1993, a Road Authority may construct and maintain
drains in, on, under, through or to any land for the purpose of draining water from, or
preventing water flowing onto, a public road.

In addition, Section 76(5) and (6) states — The owner or occupier of any land adjacent to a
public road shall take all reasonable steps to ensure that water is not prevented, obstructed
or impeded from draining into, onto, under, through or to his land from a public road. A road
authority may serve a notice in writing on the owner or occupier of any land adjacent to a
public road requiring him to carry out specified works or take specified measures to ensure
that water is not prevented, obstructed or impeded from draining into, onto, under, through
or to his land from a public road.

8. Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor J. Scanlan

1 will move at the next Meeting that the Director of Service, Transportation, Mobility and
Public Realm, to trail average speed cameras along the R520 and R522 and to revert to
our Municipal Council with the outcome. Findings are needed in the interest of safety.

The Motion was seconded by Councillor Collins

In proposing the Motion, Councillor Scanlan expressed dissatisfaction at the response
received and referred to the major issue of speeding and safety on the R520 and R522 at
various locations. A discussion took place on the use of Go Safe vans, identifying locations
and the static speed camera on the N69. Following discussion, it was agreed that this matter
be placed as an Item on the Agenda for discussion at the next Meeting with the
Superintendent and An Garda Siochana.

REPLY:

The use of average speed cameras is particularly effective on high speed, low conflict roads
for example Motorways, National roads and Tunnels.

To date in Ireland the use of average speed cameras has been confined to the following
locations:

¢ Dublin Port Tunnell
® M7 (J26 Nenagh to J27 Birdhill) since 2022
¢ Further rollouts were on N3 (Cavan), N4 (Mayo) and N2 (Meath) in 2024 and 2025.

The reason for the deployment to those locations were that they were located along higher
speed link roads with a notable collision history.

The use of average speed cameras is less effective where there are frequent junctions or
accesses and therefore is not suitable for much of our Regional Road network. Furthermore
the upfront costs for this form of speed reduction mechanism is significant €150k - €250k,
therefore future deployment onto our Regional road network will be based on measured
collision history, speed data and road geometry.

10. Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor L. Galvin

| will move at the next Meeting that the Council includes grass collection to all contracts
for all estates in the District of Newcastle West.

The Motion was seconded by Councillor Collins.

In proposing the Motion, Councillor Galvin requested that the collection of grass following
grass cutting would be included in the contract for grass cutting due to grass on driveways
following grass cutting and grass potentially blocking surface water drains. Following
discussion on costing and substantial increase in costs for grass collection to be included in
the grass cutting contract, it was agreed that the Meetings Administrator would bring this
matter to the attention of the relevant Directorate.

REPLY:

Limerick City and County Council have engaged the services of a contractor to provide the
grass cutting services for the 29.66 hectares of green areas in the charge of the Council within
the Municipal District of Newcastle West. The contract requires 16 cuts annually (every two
weeks during the growing season) at a rate of €126.50 per hectare per cut. The services does
not include collection of the cut grass.

The inclusion of grass collection in any new contract for the District would incur a rate (for cut
and collection) of €750 per hectare (2026 pricing) and a requirement for an additional
€300,000 exclusive of VAT for the Municipal District of Newcastle West alone.

Current budget allocation precludes the inclusion of grass collection as part of the grass
cutting contract. Members are advised that grass collection is not a service provided in any
District of Limerick City and County Council and the Parks Section provide the same level of
Grass cutting service level across all Districts for green areas in the charge of the Council.
