**4.Limerick City Centre**

The Marketing Officer, Economic and Tourism Development delivered a presentation

outlining the programme for the St. Patrick’s Festival 2026. Members were advised that the

festival will run from 13th to 17th March and will feature a series of events across the city and

county, including street entertainment, the 54th International Band Championship, a Night-

Time Economy open air concert at Arthur’s Quay Park, and the St. Patrick’s Day Parade

themed “Legends of Limerick.”

Championship, with six travelling from overseas, the introduction of live streaming of the

event for the first time, and details of the parade route and Grand Marshal. The programme

also includes Seachtain na Gaeilge activities, multiple events at the Limerick Milk Market,

and 13 county parades taking place from 15th to 17th March.

Members noted the extensive schedule, the strong community involvement, and the

promotional work undertaken for this year’s festival.

The Cathaoirleach asked that Members share and promote the festival through their own

social media outlets.

**REPLY:** The Stage 2 Road in Mungret was substantially completed on 29th March 2024 and

commence. Limerick City and County Council will continue to explore funding

sources for this scheme as the planning documentation is being developed.

**14.Questionsubmittedby Councillor S. Beasley**

***I**will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick how many times the***

***same potholes**in a given**area are patched before a permanent repair is carried out, in***

***particular the recurring potholes on the Abbey Bridge.***
---
**REPLY:** In the case of the pothole referred to on Abbey bridge the recurring nature of the

defect was due to it being on a bridge expansion joint and thereby vulnerable to

repeat defects due to joint movement.

During the winter period the Road maintenance crews are carrying out pothole

repairs fulltime using Delay set macadam which is a cold applied temporary patch

repair. This method is used primarily during the winter period for emergency repairs.

Where repeated emergency repairs are required it is prudent to plan a permanent

repair subject to funding being in place.

Since January 2026, the Council road maintenance crews have trialled using a Hot

Box pothole repair in which a heated container is used to transport store and

maintain hot-mix asphalt at the correct working temperatures for longer periods.

This will allow the crews to perform a more durable pothole repair.

Furthermore, the Council employ Contractors to carry out Hot Asphalt repairs on

heavily trafficked routes within the Metropolitan District subject to the availability of

funding. These permanent patch repairs are very expensive to resource.

**28.Notice of Motionsubmittedby Councillor S. Hickey O’Mara**

***I will move at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick that***

***communication boards are installed in Council run public spaces. Communication***

***Boards**assist**neuro divergent residents, those with disabilities, those experiencing ill***

***mental health, and others, to communicate their needs in public spaces. The Right to***

***Communication is a fundamental human right enshrined in the Universal Declaration of***

***Human Rights, ensuring that the communication needs of residents are upheld by the local***

***authority is crucial.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Hickey O’Mara, seconded by Councillor

Benson and agreed.

Members expressed their support for this Motion and highlighted the benefits of installing

communication boards in public spaces.

**REPLY:** Communication boards are primarily communication supports with particular benefit

to children and adults who are either pre-verbal or non-verbal and/or have

communication difficulties.

Limerick Libraries are committed to developing inclusive services, including autism

and neuro diverse friendly services throughout our network. In addition to the

award-winning sensory suite at Watch House Cross Library we have provided Sensory

Pods and sensory spaces through our branch network.

Interactive light projecting tables i.e. Magic Tables, Sensory toys, C Pens and Noise

reducing headphones are available throughout the network. We communicate these

inclusive services to users via the publication of social story which outlines in picture

format what a visitor may expect to see and experience at the Library. This booklet is

available throughout the network.

To date we have not received requests for communication boards from those using

the library service. The Culture department will assess this request for

communication boards together with those using our services, in co-operation with

the organisations and charities who are promoting this initiative and in the context of

available funding. Our understanding is that communication boards are specific to

the service being provided. This is to say that each service such as Libraries, Museum,

Gallery will require the design of a service specific communication board. Preliminary

research indicates that this new initiative was first introduced in some library

authorities nationally only in the past year.

A similar site-specific approach is being taken in other LCCC owned and operated

buildings.
---
Following discussions, it was agreed to consider implementing communication boards in

Libraries and Council buildings.
