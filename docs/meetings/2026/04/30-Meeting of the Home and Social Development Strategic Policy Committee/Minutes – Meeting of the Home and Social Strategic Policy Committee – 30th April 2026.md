**Welcome by Chairperson** **–** **Cllr. Stephen Keary**

An Cathaoirleach, Cllr. Stephen Keary opened the meeting and welcomed the members to the April

SPC meeting.

**Item 1: Confirm minutes from meeting held on 12th** **February 2026**

**Proposed:** Cllr. Sarah Kiely

**Seconded:** Cllr. Noreen Stokes

**Item 2: Matters arising from the Minutes**

No issues arising from the previous minutes.

**Item 3: SPC Work Programme**

Ms. Kathy O’Grady, Administrative Officer, gave a short presentation on the Home and Social

Development Strategic Policy Committee Work Programme for 2026-2029. A discussion took place

on the policy items. Ms. O’Grady outlined that if any members wanted to add any further policy

items to contact her in advance of the June SPC as the work programme would be finalised then.

Cllr. Elisa O’Donovan was in favour of a review of the current Homeless Action Plan and would be

interested in view an analysis of research and evidence gathered on the operation of the current

policy. Cllr. Sarah Kiely and Cllr. O’Donovon requested the circulation of this presentation and that

items added to future agendas be of a policy nature. In addition, Cllr. Kiely and Cllr. O’Donovan

requested an update on Choice Based Letting (CBL). Ms. Mary Hayes noted that CBL is currently

under development in conjunction with Business Improvement and an online system will be piloted

Iin the coming months. Ms. Hayes welcomed any input from elected members on this matter and

recognised the issue of housing offer refusals. Ms. Hayes added that if accommodation offered

reasonably meets the housing needs of the applicant and they have refused two offers they will not

be considered for social housing for one year.

Ms. Una Burns questioned if the transfer list is evaluated according to varying needs or if there is a

priority for medical cases. Additionally, Ms. Burns asked if the biggest issue for allocations is locating

larger housing stock for larger families. Ms. Hayes replied that Housing Support Services works

alongside the Construction, Maintenance and Strategy departments to provide housing solutions for

those with the highest need and prioritise exit from homelessness.

Cllr. Tom Ruddle queried if they are any units for people facing homelessness in the county. Ms.

Hayes expressed that she would be happy to work with any members of the SPC who wish to provide

input on a proposal for homelessness units in the county, reflecting these units are mainly located in

the city to allow ease of access to health and addiction services.
---
Cllr. Stephen Keary responded to Cllr. Ruddle that it would be prudent to provide council owned

homeless accommodation per municipal district as opposed to the county in general as the cost of

housing homeless individuals in privately owned hostels is considerable. Ms. Hayes requested that

any elected members who know of buildings in the County areas, please approach HSS to allow

these to be considered for acquisition under homeless accommodation.

The update was well received by the Committee.

**Item 4: Expert Groupon Homelessness andDrugAddiction**

Ms. Mary Hayes, Senior Executive Officer, delivered a presentation on the aims and objectives of the

expert working group co-chaired by Cllr. Sarah Beasley. An Expert Group was established as part of

the Mayoral Programme to address homelessness and drug addiction in Limerick. Ms. Hayes noted

that the group is working in collaboration with consultant Liam Mannix, Director of Research and

Dig and Dr. Laura Keyes of CircUL.

A series of workshops were held with stakeholders in order to generate innovative solutions that

could inform future strategies, policies and interventions. The working group intend to distribute

40 questionnaires to people with lived experiences with street homelessness to form more effective,

person-centred policies. Ms. Hayes mentioned a number of key actions currently being proposed by

the group including tailored accommodation solutions to cater for diverse needs of those

experiencing homelessness.

Mr. Mannix expressed his gratitude to work on such a beneficial project and encouraged attendees

to provide any input they may have on actions of the working group.

A number of Councillors requested a review of the current Allocation Scheme, the transfer list policy

and the Homeless Action Plan. Ms. Hayes noted they would be happy to hold a briefing with elected

members from the SPC to discuss these policies.

Ms. Hayes informed the SPC members that the Mid West Homeless Action Plan is up for review

shortly by the joint steering committee, which includes the HSE, TUSLA, Clare County Council and

stakeholders from the AHB sector.

This presentation was well received by the committee.

**Item 5: Case Study- TurnkeyAcquisitions**

Ms. Kathy O’Grady, Administrative Officer, gave a presentation to seek SPC policy guidance on

whether Limerick City and County Council should continue to acquire completed or near-complete

residential units in private developments where a portion of units have already been sold on the

open market and the developer is unable to sell the remaining units due to no bids. This applies to

housing schemes outside of the Limerick City Metropolitan District in small towns and villages where

finding purchasers for completed units may be more difficult due to demand and location. Key
---
considerations were discussed including housing need and delivery, vacancy and dereliction risk and

market impact and perception.

**That Limerick City and County Council Housing Department continues to acquire residual units in**

**partially sold developments in settlements outside of the Limerick City Metropolitan District**

**subject to:**

•

**Value for money (i.e., independent market valuation)**

•

**Ensuring last units purchased of a scheme are for either Incremental Sale or Affordable**

**Sale subject to funding approval and AHF eligibility to ensure an ownership model overall**

**within the small scheme**

The members agreed that the recommendation should proceed to the next Full Council meeting for

approval.

Cllr. Stephen Keary queried the difference between Incremental Sale and Affordable Sale and

whether there are income limits for either. Ms. O’Grady added that eligibility criteria for the

Incremental Purchase, required the applicant be in receipt of HAP/RAS or on the social housing list

whereas for Affordable, these properties can be purchased by private citizens. Mr. Vincent Murray

informed attendees that the income limits vary depending on the scheme in question and that both

offer a reduction in price to allow people on a variety of income levels to access home ownership.

This presentation was well received by the committee.

**Item 6: Review of Tenant Handbook**

Mr. Brendan Kidney, Senior Engineer, stated that the current Tenant Handbook was created back in

2019 and Limerick City and County Council are now looking to put a working group together to

review and update the handbook. The working group would be made up of 2 or 3 Councillors from

the SPC, the maintenance team and community sustainment staff.

Cllr. Sarah Kiely, Cllr. Sarah Beasley and Cllr. Tom Ruddle expressed an interest in putting their

names forward for the working group.

This item was deferred to the June SPC.

**Item 8: Short Update of Rightsizing Voids**

Ms. Kathy O’Grady, Administrative Officer, stated there has been no current rightsizing voids to date.

**Item 9: A Review of Future Modular Housing Development**

Mr. Darragh Quinn, Executive Architect gave a presentation on the Future of Modular Housing and

Modern Methods of Construction (MMC). The members had a lot of questions in relation to the
---
Mayoral Programme on SMART Housing and requested that a Special SPC be held in advance of the

next Home and Social Development SPC on SMART Housing. Cllr. Sarah Kiely and Cllr. Stephen Keary

acknowledged the substantial work undertaken by Mr Quinn and the wider SMART Housing team.

Cllr. Maria Donoghue and Mr. Michael Joyce, expressed their concerns at constructing homes in

haste, questioning if this leads to a shorter life expectancy for the units themselves. Mr Quinn

responded that timeline delivery is a crucial part of the construction process and that the materials

selected must meet the rigorous standards for Local Authority housing set out by the National

Standards Authority of Ireland. Mr. Joyce asked whether the HSE or private companies may consider

building housing units for their staff to contribution to housing activation.

Cllr. Azad Talukdar questioned if planning permission will then be required for modular units in

homeowner’s garden in future. Mr Quinn responded that the government are currently working on

exemption criteria to remove barriers for those interested in erecting modular homes to meet

housing needs, however, fire safety and water connections must be considered.

Members agreed to hold a Special SPC on SMART Housing in advance of the June SPC.
