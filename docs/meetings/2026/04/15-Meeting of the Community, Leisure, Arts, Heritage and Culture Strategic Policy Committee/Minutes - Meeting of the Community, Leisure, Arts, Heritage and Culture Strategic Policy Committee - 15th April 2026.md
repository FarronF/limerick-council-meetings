Item2: Matters Arising

No matters arising were brought forward,

Item 3: Update on Strategic Policy Committee work plan development

Director of Service, Sean Lenihan, informed members of the progress made on the development of
the Community, Leisure, Arts, Heritage and Culture Strategic Policy Committee Work Programme.
Members were advised that Corporate Services organised training workshops for all SPC members
on 14 January 2026, delivered by the Institute of Public Administration. Following this training, a
decision was taken to work with the Limerick SPC to schedule the planning of individual SPC Work
Programmes. A Work Programme workshop was subsequently held on 26 February 2026 and was
attended by both members and officials. It was noted that work is currently ongoing on the draft
Work Programme, which was presented to members at the meeting.

The presentations on the draft Work Programme were delivered in sequence as follows: Culture by
Damien Brady, City and County Librarian; Community and Rural Development by Karen Burke, Town
Centre First Officer; Sports by Louise Magner and Eileen Gleeson; and Heritage by Thomas O'Neill,
Heritage Officer.

In outlining the next steps, it was advised that the draft Work Programme will be circulated to all
members for observations, comments and feedback. Any feedback received from members will be
considered prior to circulating the final Work Programme. The final version will then be submitted
to the CPG for approval.

Members were advised that the Rural Development submission highlighted the importance of the
late-night economy in both urban and rural areas, alongside the need to engage with local groups
to identify barriers and opportunities.

The submission further noted that, while local planning policy recognises the importance of arts and
culture in supporting quality of place and societal! wellbeing, current levels of state investment are
insufficient to meet demand or realise full potential. Significant gaps were identified in the
availability of arts and cultural infrastructure, especially in relation to artist studio spaces and
rehearsal facilities. While some provision exists within Limerick City and limited rural locations, a
lack of facilities in parts of the county, most notably west Limerick, was highlighted. Concerns were
also raised regarding the long-term sustainability of existing and proposed studio spaces due to
limited funding for ongoing maintenance and operational costs.

Challenges associated with the operation of public-facing cultural venues with intermittent use were
also outlined. The submission identified co-location and multi-use of spaces as a more sustainable
approach and highlighted the temporary or adaptive use of vacant buildings as a viable solution,
referencing previous successful initiatives in Limerick. The submission recommended the
development of 2 coordinated and funded national strategy for arts and cultural infrastructure,
increased direct revenue support for local authority-led arts facilities, support for multi-use cultural
hubs, and dedicated resourcing for cultural needs assessments as part of development planning

processes.

Members thanked the Town Centre First Officer for the update and emphasised the importance of
protecting late-night venues through appropriate planning and zoning policies. It was noted that a
number of venues have closed in recent years due to complaints from nearby residential
developments. Members also highlighted the need to ensure culture is embedded within
regeneration areas and to encourage greater collaboration so that arts and cultural activity can
reach a wider audience.

While acknowledging the value of purpose-built venues, members noted that such spaces can be
intimidating for some audiences. The benefits of shared use of spaces and looking beyond
traditional arts venues were highlighted, as this approach could be mutually beneficial for
businesses and audiences alike. Concerns were also raised regarding the lack of rehearsal space,
particularly for young people, the limited availability of housing for artists, and ongoing insurance
challenges faced by venues.

Director of Service, Sean Lenihan, advised that there may be a further opportunity to use another
