4. REGENERATION, COMMUNITY R' DEVE ENT DIRECTORATE
Presentation on the Community Response Centre project.

The Cathaoirleach welcomed Ms Anne Rizzo, A/Senior Executive Officer, Community to the
Meeting.

She proceeded to outline details of the proposed Community Response Centre project,
which was being rolled out across all local authorities, including Limerick City & County. This
Community Response Centre project was in response to severe weather events that had
occurred in Ireland in recent years. The Community Response Centre project was an entirely
new concept where basic essential services (power, water, broadband, hot food, showers)
could be provided to communities following a severe weather event or other such major
issue.

The proposal was that sports halls and community centre’s would be availed of as familiar
places in the community, where locals could meet up and ask for help or offer to assist
others. Each centre would be supported by the various agencies including Local Authorities,
Civil Defence, Red Cross, HSE, An Garda Siochana, etc.

She outlined how it was proposed that Community Response Centres would be embedded
in the frameworks for Emergency Management; would be volunteer led; and would be
generally for communities without services for a period approaching 72 hours. They would
be supported by Local Authorities and other agencies and would be “beacons” in a
community when normal day-to-day life is disrupted by an absence of key services or
communication tools.

She confirmed that the Department of Rural and Community Development and the
Gaeltacht had supplied significant capital funding to Local Authorities to procure
transportable generators suitable to maintain an uninterrupted power source to a
designated Community Support Centre.

Each LA will procure, store and maintain four transportable generators, 3no. 100KvA & 1no.
30KvA. These generators are identical and interchangeable, so there will be a national fleet
of 124 generators available. In response to an emergency, a local authority will decide which
Community Support Centres to activate. If they have no power, the local authority can
deploy one of its generators to allow the centre to operate. She confirmed that
procurement was currently underway, and it was anticipated that these generators would
be in place in the first half of 2026. Assistance would also be provided to approved centres
to ensure that such centres could connect up to portable generators if needed.

She explained that online Expressions of Interest were being sought from volunteer groups
at present. An online app was being developed for volunteer groups to submit the details of
their Community Support Centre and there will be an online questionnaire that provides the
required information to each individual LA. The information will then be stored in a GIS
application that each LA can access and, when required, the interactive GIS Map can be
shared with the public, giving the details of each activated Community Support Centre and
when the centre is due to be operational. She asked that the Elected Members would raise
awareness of this new initiative in their local communities.

The Cathaoirleach and Elected Members thanked Ms Rizzo for the update and welcomed the
fact that these measures were being put in place for severe weather events. They also
welcomed the fact that portable generators would be provided to each local authority so as
to assist when such weather events occur. They agreed to circulate details of the application
process for the Community Response Centres to their wider community contacts, so that
those interested could register their interest.

QUESTIONS

6. Question submitted by Councillor O’Sullivan

| will ask at the next meeting of the Cappamore-Kilmallock Municipal District that following
recent damage to Ballaghaderg Bridge (Mitchelstown Golf Club) has Limerick City and
County Council engaged with Cork County Council to access any structural damage to the
underside of the Bridge.

Reply:

As part of the next stage of the assessment process, engagement will be initiated with the
Bridge Section of Cork County Council to arrange a site meeting within the forthcoming
two-week period. The purpose of this meeting will be to determine the appropriate actions
required and to establish which Local Authority will assume the lead role in progressing the
matter. Following this engagement, a detailed maintenance timeline and programme will be
prepared and finalised.
