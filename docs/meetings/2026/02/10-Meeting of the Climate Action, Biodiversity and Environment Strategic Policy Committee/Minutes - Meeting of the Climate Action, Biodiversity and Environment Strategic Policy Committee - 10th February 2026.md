Item 4 - Feasibility Study for New Waste Management System for Limerick City Centre –Stakeholder

Engagement - KEO Consultants – Edward Wilkins and Cristina Harber.

KEO Consultants presented an update on the Feasibility Study for a new Waste Management System

for Limerick City Centre. The presentation outlined proposals to improve waste storage and

presentation, increase participation by residents and businesses in waste prevention and recycling,

streamline collection systems, and support the achievement of EU recycling and circular economy

targets.

Members discussed the chronic issues associated with Bring Bank and Clothes Bank sites, particularly

persistent illegal dumping, overflowing banks, and the negative impact on local environments and

nearby communities. Specific reference was made to ongoing problems at certain city locations,

including Dock Road, and concerns regarding public health, visual amenity, and rodent activity.

Several of members highlighted that Clothes Banks, rather than standard Bring Banks, were the

primary source of difficulty, noting infrequent collections, unauthorised scavenging, and misuse of

sites. Suggestions were made that problematic Clothes Banks should be removed, relocated, or

consolidated at more controlled locations such as recycling centres. Other members cautioned

against removal without prior community consultation and emphasised the importance of

maintaining accessible recycling facilities.

The use of CCTV and enforcement measures at Bring Bank locations was discussed. Officials advised

that CCTV was operational at several sites and was assisting in enforcement, with a significant number

of fixed payment notices issued. Members agreed that these measures should be given time to take

effect before more permanent decisions were made regarding removal of banks.

Following discussion, it was agreed that:

•

CCTV monitoring and other mitigation measures would continue at problematic Bring Bank

locations.
---
•

The operation and performance of Clothes Banks would be kept under review, particularly at

sites with persistent dumping.

•

No immediate removal of Clothes Banks would take place, pending further assessment.

•

The issue would be revisited at a future SPC meeting as part of the ongoing waste

management review.

•

The presentation would be circulated to all members.

The Chairperson and Director of Service thanked the presenters and acknowledged the value of the

study in informing future policy decisions, including the planned review of Waste Presentation

Byelaws.

Item 5 – The Million Trees Project – Can tree planting provide a sustainable, long-lasting

environmental legacy to the Ryder Cup – Aidan Finn, Senior Engineer.

Mr. Aidan Finn, Senior Engineer, gave a presentation on the Million Trees Project, a large-scale native

tree-planting initiative being progressed in the context of the Ryder Cup 2027. The project aims to

establish approximately 100 acres of native woodland by 2027, with tree planting to take place

primarily within County Limerick, where feasible.

Members were advised that 20 acres are to be planted at Adare Manor, with the Council seeking

additional suitable lands to achieve the overall target. Potential participating landowners must have

land parcels of 10 acres or more. All proposed sites will be assessed by the Forestry Service for

suitability, and eligible landowners may avail of grant aid and annual premium payments, subject to

the planting of trees of native provenance. The project will manage site assessment and planting, with

landowners required only to make the land available.

During discussion, members welcomed the environmental benefits of large-scale tree planting but

stressed the importance of accuracy in describing the project’s climate impact. Concerns were raised

regarding the use of the term “carbon offsetting”, with members noting that the scale of planting

proposed would not fully offset the carbon footprint associated with a major international event. It

was emphasised that the initiative should be presented as a positive contribution to biodiversity

enhancement and climate mitigation rather than as a complete offset.

In response, it was clarified that the initiative is formally titled the Million Trees Project, and that it

forms part of a broader suite of environmental measures, alongside future initiatives in areas such as

energy efficiency, water conservation, and waste minimisation.

The Chairperson thanked Mr. Finn for the presentation, and it was agreed that the presentation

would be circulated to all members.
