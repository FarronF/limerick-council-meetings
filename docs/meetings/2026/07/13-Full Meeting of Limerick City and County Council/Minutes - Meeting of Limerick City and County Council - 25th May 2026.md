Unaudited Annual Financial Statement 2025 – Limerick City and County

Council

Circulated, Unaudited Annual Financial Statement for year ended 31st December, 2025, for

Limerick City and County Council, together with report from the Director General dated 13th

May, 2026.

The Director, Finance, Investment and Governance gave a presentation in relation to the

Unaudited Annual Financial Statement 2025. He advised that Members were requested to

consider the Annual Financial Statement under Section 108 of the Local Government Act 2001

(as amended). He outlined that total Revenue Expenditure for 2025 amounted to €915.73

million and total Revenue Income amounted to €915.74 million.

The Director further outlined that capital expenditure for 2025 amounted to €342.17 million,

while capital income amounted to €364.06 million. He informed the Members that, grants

accounted for €312.05 million of total capital income and that the closing capital account

balance on 31st December 2025 stood at €113.92 million.

The Director noted that the Balance Sheet showed Fixed Assets of €3.52 billion as of 31st

December 2025. He further noted that collection rates continued to perform strongly, with

commercial rates collection reaching 97%, housing rents collection 93% and housing loans

collection 90%.

The Director highlighted that payroll costs remained the Council’s most significant

expenditure at €105.59 million in 2025, reflecting the impact of national pay agreements.  He

acknowledged the continued efforts of staff and the support of the Elected Members in

maintaining the Council’s strong financial position and delivering value for money and

efficiencies across the organisation.

Members thanked the Director for his presentation and welcomed the positive financial

position reflected in the Unaudited Annual Financial Statement. Members commended

Management and Staff on the excellent rates collection in 2025, and acknowledged the work

undertaken to maintain a balanced budget and revenue surplus. They queried if the Director

General and the Mayor were satisfied with the report.

The Director General advised that he was satisfied to recommend the Unaudited Annual

Financial Statement 2025 to the Council and thanked the Elected Members and Staff for their

contribution in maintaining the Council’s financial stability and delivery of services throughout

the year.

Question submitted by Councillor E. O’Donovan

***I will ask the Mayor for an update on the actions and progress of the Transport Sub-Group***

***in relation to the ongoing delays to the BusConnects programme in Limerick. To ask***

***specifically:***

***- Whether the Minister for Transport and NTA has attended, or has been invited to attend,***

***any meeting of the Transport Sub-Group to discuss the delays and future delivery timelines***

***for BusConnects in Limerick; and***

***- Whether any progress has been made in relation to my request, raised at the September***

***2025 meeting of Limerick City and County Council, for Bus Route No. 1 to service the Mulcair***

***Road area.***

**REPLY**: BusConnects is part of the agreed work programme of the Limerick Project Ireland

2040 Transport Sub-Group, which meets quarterly and has had BusConnects as a

substantive agenda item.

The NTA is a member of the Sub-Group, and at the meeting of 12 February 2026, gave

a direct update on BusConnects, confirming no change to the programme since the

previous meeting. They advised that some design work would begin earlier than

planned, and that, due to a lack of funding, no new bus services could be delivered in

Limerick in 2026, with new services possible in 2027 subject to funding. Delays and
---
16

delivery timelines have therefore been discussed directly with the NTA at Sub-Group

level.

The Minister for Transport is not a member of the Sub-Group and has not attended a

meeting to date, but has accepted an invite to attend the meeting in December.

The main cause of delay is funding: the November 2025 NDP Sectoral Investment Plan

for Transport identified €500m–€1bn for the region but no specific funding for

BusConnects infrastructure in Limerick. As a result, bus services are being delivered

on a phased basis and the dedicated bus corridor infrastructure is currently delayed

to 2030. In relation to Bus Route No. 1, the BusConnects Service Planning Section of

the National Transport Authority (NTA) has undertaken a high-level review of the

provision of Bus Route 1 services directly to the Mulcair Road area.

Arising from this assessment, it has been determined that further detailed modelling

is required. This modelling will examine both local service provision and the potential

impacts on the wider network redesign.

The NTA Design Team will progress this additional analysis and is expected to provide

an update on the completion of the initial modelling stages in the coming weeks.

Councillor O’Donovan welcomed the response and noted the confirmation that the NTA had

undertaken a review of the provision of Bus Route 1 services to the Mulcair Road area.

Members expressed concern regarding delays in the rollout of BusConnects, particularly the

deferral of the bus corridor infrastructure works until 2030.

The Mayor emphasised the importance of cross-party support for the delivery of BusConnects

in Limerick. It was agreed that the Mayor’s petition seeking the accelerated delivery of

BusConnects would be circulated to Members.
