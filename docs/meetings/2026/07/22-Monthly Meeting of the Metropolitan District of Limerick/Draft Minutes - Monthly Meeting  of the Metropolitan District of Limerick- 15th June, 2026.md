**4.Limerick City Centre**

The Director of Services, Metropolitan District of Limerick gave an update to the Members on

the ongoing works to Laneways in Limerick City Centre.

He noted that, while laneways are an important part of the city’s Georgian heritage they have

faced challenges, particularly in relation to waste management, littering and presentation of

waste.

The Director of Services highlighted that significant progress had been made through the

support of the Elected Members, businesses, Council investment, pilot projects, improved

cleansing and stronger enforcement of litter bye-laws. He advised that several laneways had

already seen substantial improvements.

He informed Members that works carried out included resurfacing, improvements to public

lighting, installation of bin storage units, installation of bollards to prevent unauthorised

parking. He further advised that additional improvement works were planned to laneways,

with a number of projects targeted for completion by the end of July.

The Director of Services referred to the Irish Business Against Litter (IBAL) June 2026 report,

which identified a number of blackspots within the city centre. He confirmed that targeted

actions were underway to address these areas through increased enforcement. In particular,

he sought the support of Members for the removal of the textile bank on the Dock Road. The

Members acknowledged the importance of textile recycling banks in the city; however, they

supported the Director’s proposal to remove the textile bank on the Dock Road.
---
Members welcomed the update and acknowledged the work of the Director of Services,

Metropolitan District of Limerick, and the City Centre Response Team. They commended the

team for the positive work undertaken and the significant improvements delivered, which had

enhanced the appearance of the city centre over the past year. Members thanked the

Cathaoirleach for making the city centre a priority for his term as Cathaoirleach of the

Metropolitan District and emphasised the importance that this focus would continue for the

year ahead.

**16.Questionsubmittedby Councillor Donoghue**
---
***I**will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick for a full breakdown***

***of footfall figures across Limerick City as previously presented by Limerick City and County***

***Council, including days and times in certain locations, demographics, and events.***

**REPLY:**Limerick City and County Council utilises a combination of different technologies to

monitor footfall and movement patterns across the city, including Micro-Radar sensors

and third-party mobility data providers.

Footfall data is collected through anonymised, non-personal measurement

technologies.

Micro-Radar sensors record movement counts only, and data is available for certain

locations across Limerick City where these sensors have been installed. The locations

were selected based on operational requirements, for example to monitor pedestrian

and traffic activity, support urban planning and transport initiatives, and to measure

economic activity in key areas.

In addition, third-party mobility datasets, generated based on mobile phone signals,

have been procured by Digital Services that provide movement and dwell-time data

for the city centre in aggregated and anonymised formats.

It is important to note that none of the footfall datasets capture personally identifiable

information, while the Council also does not collect demographic data in any of its

footfall datasets.

The data in these footfall datasets can be provided broken down by location, date, day

of the week, and time of the day, while footfall trends can be analysed in the context

of major events, seasonal variations, weather patterns, and city centre initiatives.

This enables the identification of peak and off-peak periods, and supports planning

and event analysis where required.

Given the types of data sources, the variety of methods in which the data is collected

and recorded, and the different structures of the datasets that we have available for

the city centre, it is difficult to provide that data in a standardised single dataset broken

down by location, events etc. Instead, Digital Services can provide reports and analysis

of footfall data for specific locations and timeframes upon request, subject to the

availability of valid data for those locations.

Meanwhile, Digital Services continues its efforts on behalf of the Council to enhance

its data capabilities and make reliable data available on-demand through dashboards

to support evidence-based decision making in areas such as urban planning, transport,

tourism and economic development, while ensuring strict compliance with data

protection requirements.

**25.Notice of Motion submitted by CouncillorO’Donovan**

***I**will move at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick that a plan is***

***developed to address the significant disparity in CCTV coverage between Limerick City West***

***and the other Metropolitan Local Electoral Areas, and for increased and additional CCTV***

***investment in Limerick City West.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor O’Donovan, seconded by Councillor Hickey-

O’Mara and agreed.

Members requested that a briefing be provided by Digital Services on CCTV coverage within

the Metropolitan District.

**REPLY:**The rollout of Community CCTV in Limerick has not been planned based on

Metropolitan Local Electoral Areas. Rather, the network has built up over the last

approximately thirty years in response to specific public safety issues, particularly

issues related to the sustained organised crime and gang feuding that was prevalent in

Limerick City in the late 1990s and into the 2000s.

During that time, Limerick saw very high levels of serious and violent crime which were

heavily concentrated in certain parts of the city. In response to that, CCTV systems

were installed in the areas where this activity was highest, areas which now fall within

the North and East Local Electoral Areas. The systems were installed by the Council,

working closely with An Garda Síochána and local communities. All schemes were

authorised by the Garda Commissioner and continue to operate, playing an important

role in supporting Garda investigations to this day. The CCTV network as it currently

exists reflects those historic decisions made during periods of significant organised

crime, rather than anything recent or planned across LEAs.
---
Proposals for new CCTV installations should be brought forward on the basis of

identified risks and patterns of criminal behaviour, rather than based on geographical

location only. To be considered for approval, a proposal must:

• meet the requirements around necessity, justification and proportionality, as

set out under Part 5, Section 28 of the Garda Siochána (Recording Devices) Act

2023,

• have support from the community, local councillors, and An Garda Síochána,

and

• have sufficient funding in place to cover the installation costs of the CCTV

system as well as the costs of maintaining the system in the years after

installation.

The application and approval processes are resource-heavy and typically take multiple

years to complete, but if successful then end with sign-off from the Garda

Commissioner.

As an example, a new CCTV scheme was approved earlier this year for the Limerick

City West Local Electoral Area in the People’s Park. This proposal was driven by clear,

evidence-based public safety concerns linked to consistent reports of drug dealing and

violent incidents in and around the area. The application arising from this proposal

took approximately 10 months to put together, was submitted in October 2024, and

approved by the Garda Commissioner in February 2026.

In summary, while there is a disparity in the number of CCTV cameras in the Limerick

City West Local Electoral Area when compared to Limerick City East and Limerick City

North, this did not arise because of a planned rollout of the CCTV network based on

geographical boundaries. Rather, the CCTV network as it exists today has developed

over time as a result of a targeted response by Limerick City and County Council,

working with communities, local councillors, and An Garda Siochána, to organised

crime in areas that saw the highest level of this criminal activity over a sustained period

of time.
