4. ENVIRONMENT, CLIMATE ACTION AND FIRE DIRECTORATE

Presentation on Review of Grants available for Energy Upgrades

The Cathaoirleach welcomed the Climate Action Co-Ordinator, and the Energy Officer, to
the Meeting. The Energy Officer proceeded to outline details of the grants available from
the Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland (SEA!) for Energy Efficiency measures.

He outlined how there were four grant categories operated by the SEAI and these were for
Home (Residential); Business; Solar PV grants; and Electric Vehicle (Car & Charger) grants.
He then went into the detail of the grants available under each of the grant categories and
he explained the requirements under each scheme and the measures involved.

He confirmed that an additional grant of €5,000 was also being introduced by the SEAI from
the 1% July 2026 for people who traded in their car if it was 13 years or older and purchased
a new electric vehicle instead. This was a trial scheme being introduced by the Government,
with a limited initial allocation of grant funding for same.

He then spoke about the recently introduced SEAI Windows & Doors Grants Scheme and
confirmed that the scheme was for properties built before the 31° December 2010. Attic
and wall insulation also had to be of a certain minimum standard and the property also had
to meet a certain heat loss indicator vaiue.

The Cathaoirleach and Elected Members thanked him for the presentation, and they
welcomed the fact that there were so many grant category options now available in relation
to various types of energy related improvements and upgrades.

However, they agreed that some of these were expensive to install and would take a period
to pay for themselves. It was also difficult financially for those on lower incomes, to access a
lot of the grant options that were available. They also noted that the SEA! Windows and
Doors Grant Scheme had certain restrictions to it that would make it more difficult to access.
Some SEAI grant schemes also had a long lead in time from application to completion which
was not ideal.

6. jon submi by Councillor T:

| will ask at the next Meeting of the Cappamore-Kilmallock Municipal District for
confirmation as to when preparations will commence for the next Limerick Development
Plan post 2028 and clarify the position regarding any proposal to extend the current plan to
2030. Also, are there any plans for an updated Wind Energy map for the county.

Reply:

Due to proposed legislative changes currently under consideration, it is anticipated that the
commencement of the next review of the Limerick Development Plan will be delayed until
2028. This will necessitate an extension of the current Limerick Development Plan 2022-
2028 until 2030.

The Regional Spatial and Economic Strategy (RSES) for the Southern Region is also under
review, and it is expected to be finalised in Q4 2027. As the Development Plan must be
prepared in the context of an adopted RSES, its completion is a prerequisite to commencing
the Development Plan Review.

It is expected that forthcoming legislation will provide greater clarity on timelines for both
the Development Plan review and the proposed extension.

9. Notice of Motion submitted by Clir Carey
| will move at the next Meeting of the Cappamore-Kilmallock Municipal District that
designated casual trading license areas be extended to include the town of Kilmallock.

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Cllr Carey, seconded by Clir E. Ryan.

There was a discussion amongst the Elected Members in relation to the possible
introduction of a casual trading area in Kilmallock. The members agreed that the demand
for same would have to be examined, and the views of existing businesses who had many
overheads including rent, rates, etc in the town would have to be canvassed. There were
also currently several vacant retail units in the town and the utilisation of these could be a
possible consideration too.

Some Members referred to the tradition of Market Towns where people traded various
produce over the years, and that Kilmallock had been a Market Town itself in the past. They
agreed that any possible casual trading area in a town should be an addition to, rather than
displacement of, existing commercial activity in the town.

Issues in relation to the identification of a suitable possible location of a casual trading area
in Kilmallock; implications for traffic flow and parking; having appropriate planning
permission for same, insurance cover, etc. were also important to consider.


---
*<small>Scanned page, text may contain errors. See original file for clarity</small>*  

It was agreed to arrange a Workshop with the relevant staff involved in the area to discuss
the matter in more detail and tease out the issues and process involved.

Reply:

Casual Trading in Ireland is governed by the Casual Trading Act, 1995 and associated
regulations and byelaws (made by the local authority). The purpose of the legislation is to
regulate casual trading, which is defined as the selling goods at a place (including a public
road) to which the public have access as of right or at any other place that is a casual trading
area,

The designation of a casual trading area is a reserved function of the elected members and
would require extensive consultation with the public, residents and traders in any proposed
location having regard to the potential implications for any of the preceding, e.g. potential
displacement effects for existing retailers who are ratepayers. In this regard, casual trading
areas are usually designated where there has been a tradition of this activity over many
years.

There are further implications such as the impact on parking and traffic management issues
that may be associated with any proposed location. Any person who applies for a casual
trading licence in a designated area is usually accommodated once they meet the
requirements for the licence.

At present there are 3 designated areas for casual trading in Limerick, viz: Limerick City near
the Milk Market, Newcastle West and Abbeyfeale. The operation of these markets is
supervised and regulated by Council staff who ensure compliance with the requirements of
the Council’s byelaws. Any proposal would have to be considered very carefully in the
context of the retail profile of the town and potential impacts on same.

10. Notice of Motion submitted by Clir Conway

| will move at the next meeting of Cappamore Kilmallock Municipal District that Limerick
City & County Council provide access to public libraries in areas with Deis schools in the
evenings for exam students to study for the duration of the state exams perhaps up to
9:00pm approx. from 1st June to 15"

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Cllr Conway, seconded by Cllr O’Sullivan.

The Members acknowledged the range of services and facilities provided by the Library
Services and its role as a focal point for a lot of community related events across various
demographic age groups in the community.

Reply:

The remit of Public Libraries has evolved significantly over the past two decades, largely
because of successive National Public Library Strategies. These strategies have increasingly
emphasised the role of the Library Service as a central hub within the community.

Public Libraries now fulfil a range of diverse and multifaceted functions, including:


---
*<small>Scanned page, text may contain errors. See original file for clarity</small>*  

¢ The development of the library as a multi-purpose educational and social space that
is accessible to all members of the community.

¢ The organisation of ongoing events and activities for children, such as the Children's
Book Festival and the Right to Read programme. These initiatives drive increased
footfall and are directly associated with improved literacy outcomes and higher levels
of reading engagement.

¢ The provision of events and activities for individuals and community groups,
contributing to enhanced usage and greater overall engagement with library services.

¢ The development of programmes aligned with the national "Skills for Life" framework,
with particular emphasis on digital literacy, financial literacy, sustainable living, and
environmental awareness.

Regarding study spaces, the Library Service has consistently supported students by
providing such facilities where feasible. However, the proposed focus of the Library as an
exam study space would have a detrimental impact on the delivery of other essential library
services, events, and activities. The availability of study spaces varies considerably between
library branches depending on their size and even in the larger branches Is quite limited.

While the Library Service remains committed to supporting students, it is not practicable to
dedicate the building primarily to exam study purposes without significantly affecting access
for a wide range of other users and community groups. The primary responsibility for the
provision of exam study spaces should rest with schools.

Libraries are no longer silent places and therefore the quiet that may be needed for study
purposes can in no way be guaranteed, Libraries are vibrant spaces, often with multiple
activities taking place simultaneously. These activities can range from books clubs to
conversation groups and training classes.

The period in question, in the motion, of the 1*to the 15" June would represent a challenge
as this is the actual exam period. Libraries generally experience a drop-off in in the number
of students using facilities once the exams start. To be in any way beneficial it would have to
be brought in from the Easter terms onwards, which would have severe impact for a
prolonged period on other library users, library activities and programming. This post Easter
period is busy from a programming perspective as it encompasses Spring into Storytime,
Limerick Lifelong Learning Festival and Cruinnid na nOg as well as other events.

The focus on DEIS schools, is not an easy construct. Schools are assigned as DEIS based on a
Department of Education Identification Model that assesses the socio-economic and
demographic profile of their student body. In terms of secondary schools, students often
travel outside their own area to attend school. Therefore, providing access to the nearest
library to the DEIS school may not benefit the students attending that school, as they may
live elsewhere and are commuting to and from the DEIS school on a daily basis.

8

There are currently twenty-eight post-primary schools in Limerick, eight of which are DEIS
schools. The argument to focus only on DEIS schools would be difficult to sustain. Therefore,
it would not be feasible to cater for the study needs of exam students in twenty-eight post
primary schools across the city and county. The repurposing of a public library for exam
study purposes would present a staffing challenge with the expectation that facilities would
be available up to 9pm. All our branch library staff are fully rostered to manage a frontline
service including daily openings from 10am, two late evenings to 8pm and Saturday
opening. To expand this service to 9pm daily would require a significant additional staff
resource and extra security costs, to safely manage this function. In conclusion, given the
extensive range of community services that our public libraries are already providing, the
limited size of our libraries to meet this exam study need and the additional staffing and
security requirement involved, it is not possible to repurpose the library service as an exam
study space.
