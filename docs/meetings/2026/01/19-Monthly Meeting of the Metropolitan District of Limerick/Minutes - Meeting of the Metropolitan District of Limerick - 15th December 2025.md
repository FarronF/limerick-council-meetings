**4.Limerick City Centre**

**(a)Global Active Cities**

Representatives from Limerick Sports Partnership with the aid of a presentation, provided an

update to Members on the Global Active Cities Programme. The vision of Active Cities is to

engage, maintain and increase the number of citizens, regardless of age and ability,

participating in sport and physical activity in their city.

Sport Ireland’s Active Cities project is based on the World Health Organisation's GAPPA

framework, which is focused on getting those who are least active, active.

Stakeholders involved include LCCC Parks Department, Mary Immaculate College, EU

Projects Office, HSE Health Promotion, Limerick Sports Partnership, Healthy Ireland, Public

Participation Network, Sláintecare Healthy Communities, Limerick City Partnership, Active

Travel, University of Limerick and Local Community Groups.

An example of the work Active Cities has carried out in Limerick can be seen at O'Brien Park,

Clare Street, where a collaborative development to revamp the Park took place. Since 2024,

it has had over 1,450 individual users, 275 uses per month on average, over 3,500 hours of

activity and over 35,000 people active in the park.

Members acknowledged and commended the work of the Limerick Sports Partnership,

highlighting the vital role of physical activity in promoting health and wellbeing.

**14.Questionsubmittedby Councillor S. Benson**

***I**will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick for**an update on the***

***ESIF funding and if any progress has been made in sourcing alternative funding streams***

***for vital social supports for 2026.***

**REPLY:** The Limerick Regeneration Framework Implementation Plan (LRFIP) was launched in

2013, which was a ten-year programme to deliver on social, economic and physical

objectives. Post-2023, the Regeneration, Rural & Community Development, Sport &

Recreation Directorate has continued to deliver on the objectives outlined within the

Plan with the continued support of the Department of Housing, Local Government,

and Heritage (DHLGH).

On 9 May 2025, the Department advised Limerick City and County Council that the

Economic and Social Intervention Fund (ESIF) would be phased out, with a final

tranche of funding due in 2028. While this reflects a national shift towards

mainstreaming local social-support initiatives, the Council continues to emphasise

strongly to the Department the critical importance of sustained investment in the

social dimension of regeneration.

In the years 2019-2025, 19 ESIF-funded projects or staff roles were mainstreamed

into statutory funding or sustainable self-funding.
---
In 2025, four ESIF-funded projects were awarded significant statutory funding.

However, all of these grants are of a limited duration:

• Moyross Community Enterprise Centre – Moyross Summer Youth

Employment & Leadership Programme 2025-2026: €150,000 from the

Community Safety Fund

• Our Lady of Lourdes CSG – The Beston Model: €135,877 from the Community

Safety Fund

• Engage in Education – The Compass Programme: €133,000 from the

Community Safety Fund

• University of Limerick - The Health Alliance for Practice-based Professional

Education and Engagement (HAPPEE) Project: €130,000 from the Dormant

Accounts Fund 2026

Many ESIF-funded programmes continue to play a vital role in promoting social

inclusion, strengthening community capacity, and supporting pathways to education,

training and employment in the regeneration areas. In that regard, Limerick City and

County Council remains firmly committed to supporting community organisations

through this transition period.

Accordingly, the Directorate of Regeneration, Rural & Community Development,

Sport & Recreation is actively assisting all organisations currently in receipt of ESIF

support to identify and secure alternative, sustainable funding streams, including

opportunities across other Government Departments and agencies, philanthropic

sources, and targeted national programmes. This work will continue throughout 2026

and beyond to ensure that the progress achieved through regeneration to date is

protected and built upon.

approximately 50 bus stops were upgraded across Limerick City and County as part of

the Bus Stop Enhancement Programme. The bus stop enhancements included the

installation of concrete hardstanding areas, disability access kerbing, bus stop polls,

TFI signage and road markings at official Bus Eireann and Local Link bus stops. 50 bus

stops have been recently upgraded, but only 5 bus shelters were installed prior to

April 2025.

We have been advised that Bauer Media Outdoor are expecting to have a steady

supply of bus shelters in Q2 of 2026. At present we cannot confirm how many bus

shelters will be installed in 2026.

**REPLY:** The SMART Housing Programme is a core initiative under the new Government

Housing Plan 'Delivering Homes, Building Communities 2025–2030'. It is specifically

designed to deliver affordable rental accommodation for workers who are currently

unable to secure suitable housing due to acute shortages in the private rental

market. This shortage is contributing to sky-rocketing rents in Limerick, long

commutes, extended periods living in the family home, and reliance on overcrowded

house-sharing arrangements, none of which offer a sustainable or appropriate

housing solution for working adults.

As part of the initial phase of the Programme and following a Councillor private

workshop held 25th November 2025, a number of initial Council-owned sites have

been identified for further assessment and potential delivery. The Boro Park site is

included on this preliminary list, subject to early-stage engagement with the local

community. This choice was made on the basis that the Boro Park site was zoned

suitable for residential use in the current Development Plan approved by Full Council

in 2022 and the related Colbert Quarter Spatial Framework (deemed consistent with

the Development Plan) which had contemplated approximately 500 homes from 2 to

10 storeys on the Boro Park site.

Following the appointment of design teams which is expected next week, it is the

Mayor’s stated intention to undertake structured and meaningful engagement with

the local community around the Boro Park to fully understand local considerations

with a view to progressing to full planning in line with the objectives of the Statutory

Development Plan.

The Mayor will ensure that Elected Members are kept fully informed at each stage of

this process and that any decision regarding the progression of the Boro Park site will

be made transparently, with due regard to community input and the strategic

objectives of the SMART Housing Programme.
