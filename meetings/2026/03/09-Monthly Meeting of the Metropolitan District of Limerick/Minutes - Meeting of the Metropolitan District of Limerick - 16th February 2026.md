**9.Questionsubmittedby Councillor M. Donoghue**

***I will ask at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick what stronger***

***measures have been implemented as stated in paragraph 17.4 Provisions of Event***

***Management Services in the Statutory Audit Report to the members of Limerick City and***

***County Council for year ended 31st December 2024, and what led to the significantly***

***increased spending in 2023 and 2024 to a single supplier.***

**REPLY:**The provision of Event Management Services for festivals and events are a Mayoral

function under legislation.

The following is an outline of the process being followed for tendering:

• Request for Tenders are published on the e-Tenders platform and or on the

OJEU (Official Journal of the European Union.

• Tenders are assessed by a suitably qualified assessment panel based on the

award criteria set of in the Request for Quotation document.

• Outcome documents are prepared followed by a Tender recommendation

report.

• The report is reviewed by Senior Executive Officer (SEO), and a

recommendation is made to the Director of Service (DoS). If satisfied the DoS

endorses the recommendation for Mayoral Review and Sign off.

• A Mayoral order is prepared, sign off by the Mayor.

• A Contract is signed by the appointed contractor, the Mayor of Limerick, and

is witnessed by SEO or DOS.

• Signed copies of contracts are saved digitally.
---
LCCC Civic festivals have audiences ranging from 40,000 to 110,000 people & are

primarily held in the public realm. Stakeholder engagement is central to planning and

delivery, in particular An Garda Síochana, HSE, Fire Service, Roads, Civil Defence &

others.

On occasion unforeseen costs can arise from additional Health & Safety, logistical

and/or operational reasons put forward by consultees. New legislation and/or

guidelines for large scale public events best practice can bring new requests, in

addition adapted or additional programme content may also require consideration.

No officer delegation is in place to authorise additional contract costs. Where

additional costs are approved and signed off by the Mayor a ‘Contract Modification’

is published on eTenders / OJEU, or a new tender process is carried out if required.

Overspend in 2023 and 2024 refers to contracts issued in November 2022. The

events industry was only recovering from covid pandemic in 2022, when these

tenders were issued. The industry experienced widespread inflation in 2023

particularly with security, equipment hire, insurance and staff costs. These were

largely unavoidable and impacted on budget planning and execution in 2023. Prior to

covid, there was a strong culture of volunteering for these festivals in Limerick, that

defrayed staff costs (stewarding etc). Following the pandemic, this was greatly

reduced and staff had to be hired for roles previously carried out by volunteers. Cost

increase examples include additional grandstand, visit of naval ship, and water safety

measures.

Given the economic importance of these civic festivals and events to Limerick, there

was an emphasis on creative programming and activity that also resulted in

increased costs to local authority but brought huge economic benefit to the city

(estimated at €10M) and of vital importance to reinvigorating the hospitality and

tourism industry and support of creative sector.

Contract documents have been revised to produce tighter controlled pricing for

these festivals.

**23.Notice of Motionsubmittedby Councillor J. Leddin**

***I will move at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick that Limerick City***

***and County Council provide an update and outline the next steps in the delivery of a new***

***community centre for Raheen/Dooradoyle following recommendation in the previously***

***published feasibility report completed in 2023.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Leddin, seconded by Councillor

O’Donovan and agreed.

Members emphasised the need for the provision of a community centre in the Raheen,

Dooradoyle and Mungret area, noting the significant population growth in these

communities. It was agreed that an update and timelines on plans for a new community

centre in the area would be provided at the April Metropolitan District Meeting.

**REPLY:**A report was commissioned by Limerick City & County Council in 2022, to conduct a

feasibility study for a Multi-purpose Community Centre for the

Dooradoyle/Mungret/Raheen area. The report was prepared by O’Raw Consultancy.

The main recommendations from that report were:

1. Dooradoyle/Raheen should be considered in its own right for the provision of

a community centre in that geographic area.

2. Phase two of the feasibility study consider location and design for

Dooradoyle/Raheen as a distinct community for the purposes of community

centre development.

3. The Consultants recommended that a sports hall, café, suite of rooms, IT

facilities, should all be considered as essential in the development of

community facilities in the area.

Due to Dooradoyle / Raheen being a very built-up area, there are few options with

regard to suitable sites for a community centre. The Regeneration, Community, Sport

and Rural Development Directorate has consulted with other directorates of Limerick

City & County Council who have also expressed an interest in the preferred site at St.

Nessans Road, Dooradoyle. Once the site selection process has been completed, the

Regeneration, Community, Sport and Rural Development directorate will progress

the next phase of the project i.e. a feasibility study of the site.

***and County Council undertake a traffic study on the Groody Road, Castletroy to take***

***Campus, Groody Road, reference 25/60113 and for future developments. In addition to***

agreed.

Members highlighted the need to include the entire Castletroy and Annacotty area in the

study, noting that the existing infrastructure is insufficient to accommodate the scale of

residential development already built and currently proposed.

**REPLY:**It is acknowledged that traffic in the Groody/Ballysimon area is congested

particularly at peak times. A significant objective of the transport strategy and the

development plan is to promote modal shift and sustainable development

particularly where infrastructure provides for this.

Any proposed development in this area should be aligned with the Development

Plan and car parking provision for any development is therefore approved at a

reduced rate to the maximum standard as appropriate. Significant cycle parking is

also important.

Considering these factors, any significant applications must also prepare a mobility

management plan which is reviewed with the Council regularly. This mobility plan

requires developers to report on modal use by a development, including traffic

figures using the development.

**32.Notice of Motionsubmittedby Councillor S. Hickey O’Mara**

***I will move at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick that, as a way of***

***promoting youth engagement with local culture, a pilot scheme is developed that offers***

***young people living in Limerick aged 18-22 the opportunity to apply for a Local Culture***

***Voucher from Limerick Arts Office, of an amount to be agreed, which they can use to***

***attend events at participating local cultural venues. Cultural venues can then redeem***

***these vouchers from the Limerick Arts Office.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Hickey O’Mara, seconded by Councillor

O’Donovan and agreed.

In moving the Motion, Councillor Hickey O’Mara outlined the need to provide opportunities

to younger people to attend local cultural events.

**REPLY:** Limerick City and County Council aim to improve access to arts and culture

opportunities for young people, through initiatives such as supporting youth theatre

and youth choir projects, culture night and programmes for youth. The form of

supports currently available under the remit the arts office are clearly defined grant

and bursary supports to individuals and organisations, contributions to strategic

organisations and programmes agreed under the Arts Council Local Authority

partnership programme. The proposed voucher system as suggested is, in effect, a

request for a new Limerick City and County Council funding stream, to encourage

access to cultural venues for 18 to 22 year olds. The arts office currently does not

have the capacity to support or facilitate a vouchers provision system which would

subsidise attendance at events.

Cultural venues under the auspices of the City and County Council, such as the

Limerick City Gallery and the Limerick Museum provide opportunities for

engagement and encourage access through free entry. Cultural venues in receipt of

Limerick City and Council annual funding are requested to report on audience

numbers and to seek to develop audiences. These could be asked to consider

including promotional work to support access to their venues by 18 to 22 year olds,

within their marketing and access strategies.

**33.Notice of Motionsubmittedby Councillor E. O’Donovan**

***I will move at the next meeting of the Metropolitan District of Limerick that Traffic***

***Management run a high visibility campaign and presence to address parking enforcement***

***issues in residential areas outside of the core central business district.***

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor O’Donovan, seconded by Councillor

Hickey O’Mara and agreed.

In moving the Motion, Councillor O’Donovan outlined the frustration experienced by

residents living outside the core business district and highlighted the need for increased

patrols in these areas to tackle illegal parking.

**REPLY:** Limerick City and County Council and An Garda Síochána launched the “Think Before

You Park” campaign in Jan 2024. This campaign is a city and county wide public

awareness campaign with enforcement by An Garda Siochana. The campaign has

been run annually since 2024. The Council continues to assign a Traffic Warden with

a vehicle to carry out patrols and enforcement duties throughout residential areas

outside of the central business district. This Traffic Warden focuses mainly on

residential areas near the city’s three third level colleges and University Hospital

Limerick. In addition, a Traffic Warden and the University of Limerick’s Community

Liaison Officer carry out joint visits to residential areas near the university which are

experiencing problems with illegal parking to encourage good parking practice by

residents. The Council will examine ways to promote better parking practice via its

social media channels.
