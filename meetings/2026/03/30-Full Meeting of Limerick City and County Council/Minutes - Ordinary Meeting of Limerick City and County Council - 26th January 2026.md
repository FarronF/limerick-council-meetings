Part 8 – Ryder Cup Temporary Park and Ride Facilities

The A/Senior Engineer, Ryder Cup, gave a presentation to Members outlining details of the

proposed temporary Park and Ride facilities to be provided at Mackey Roundabout,

Patrickswell and Croagh. He advised that the Government is the main stakeholder for Ryder

Cup 2027, and that Limerick City and County Council is one of twenty-four stakeholders

involved in the project, working in partnership with the National Transport Authority (NTA),

An Garda Síochána and other agencies.

He outlined the key objectives in selecting the proposed sites:
---
8

• The importance of proximity to Adare Manor, where the Ryder Cup will be hosted.

• The requirement to provide 25,000 car parking spaces, noting that lands needed to be

of suitable scale to accommodate this volume.

• Consideration was given to sites that were successfully used in the past.

• The importance of land quality, particularly in the event of poor weather conditions

during the week of the event.

The A/Senior Engineer, Ryder Cup, advised that the National Transport Authority will be

carrying out a tender for the provision of buses to and from the Park and Ride facilities.

He further advised that the majority of attendees travelling to the event in Adare will arrive

prior to tee-off at 7.00 am and will remain at the event until later in the evening. He noted

that these times are outside peak traffic times and are not expected to impact on daily traffic.

National Planning Framework Implementation: Housing Growth

Requirements

To note the circulated, Report of the Senior Planner, Planning, Heritage and Ryder Cup dated

19th January, 2026, and enclosed Mayor’s Report on the NPF Implementation: Housing

Growth Requirements on foot of the “NPF Implementation: Housing Growth Requirements”

Guidelines for Planning Authorities (July 2025) issued under Section 28 of the Planning and

Development Act 2000, as amended.

The Senior Planner, Planning, Heritage and Ryder Cup outlined that the Mayor’s Report was

prepared in response to the Section 28 Guidelines – National Planning Framework

Implementation Housing Growth Requirements issued by the Department of Housing, local

Government and Heritage issued on the 29th July 2025. The Members were advised that Local

Authorities were required to review adequacy of existing zoned lands to cater for the housing

growth requirements as set out in the Section 28 Guidelines for the duration of the current

adopted Development Plan.

The Senior Planner gave a presentation to Members on the NPF Implementation: Housing

Growth Requirements (July 2025) outlining the new Annual Housing Growth Requirements

for each Local Authority across the country, based on the updated population projections.

The key requirements and objectives were outlined as follows:

• The new target maintains the same annual housing requirement of 2,599 for Limerick

up to 2034.

• Between 2035 and 2040, the annual housing target will increase by 141 units, bringing

the target to 2,740 units per annum.

• An additional provision of up to 50% above the housing growth requirement for each

Local Authority is provided for within the Development Plan.

• The Development Plan must provide for sufficient zoned lands to cater for 10,758

housing units to the end of the plan.
---
11

The Senior Planner further advised that the Mayor’s Report recommends that a Variation to

the Limerick Development Plan be carried out to make provisions for additional zoned land

suitable for residential use within Limerick city and suburbs.

She outlined that a non-statutory public expression of interest, seeking submissions from

landowners wishing to have their land considered for residential zoning, was held in

December 2025. She noted that these submissions would now be reviewed by the Forward

Planning Team.

Members welcomed the Mayor’s recommendation that a Variation of the Limerick

Development Plan would be carried out, acknowledging the need to deliver housing in

Limerick. They stressed the importance of ensuring sustainable growth within communities

when considering the rezoning of lands. Members highlighted the lack of service capacity in

many rural areas across Limerick, which affects the ability to support housing development in

these communities.

Question submitted by Councillor E. Secas

***I will ask the Mayor what direct engagement, if any, he has had to date with the National***

***Transport Authority and relevant Government Departments or officials to progress the***

***provision of a Park and Ride facility at the Mackey Roundabout, in order to address ongoing***

***traffic congestion and associated road safety concerns; and whether he will commit to***

***further engagement with these bodies to help accelerate the delivery of this project.***

**REPLY**: I thank the Councillor for raising this issue and for continuing to highlight the

importance of the Mackey Roundabout and the associated Park and Ride proposal,

particularly in the context of traffic congestion and road safety.

This objective is explicitly reflected in my Mayoral Programme, Objective ML88.

“ML88 – Have a fully developed (if not implemented in part) plan to resolve vehicular

transport access issues at UL, Plassey Technological Park and the Mackey roundabout.

We will complete the west side access road to Moyross, parking for the duration of

this Programme any further consideration of the final and east-west central section of

the Northern Distributor Road”.

In terms of engagement, I have raised the Mackey Park and Ride and wider Mackey

Roundabout constraints in my discussions with the National Transport Authority and

with Government Departments, most notably during the preparation of submissions

under the National Development Plan process, including Project Ireland 2040. On

those occasions, I explicitly set out Limerick’s transport priorities and reiterated the

strategic importance of the Mackey location.

More generally, engagement at official level with the NTA, Transport Infrastructure

Ireland and the Department of Transport is ongoing and is led on a day-to-day basis
---
13

by the Council’s Transport and Mobility team, who have been working with these

bodies for a number of years to progress planning for a strategic Park and Ride and

mobility hub at Mackey.

I would note the Part 8 before the Full Council for the use of the location as a park and

ride for the Ryder Cup. I would hope that its becoming a reality even for this

temporary purpose will help the case for a more permanent site.

While progress has not moved at the pace any of us would like, the project remains

an active priority, and I am hopeful that agreement can be reached with the key

stakeholders, including the NTA, TII and the Department of Transport in 2026.

I will continue to use my role to support and reinforce this work at political level and

to press the relevant agencies and Departments to advance the project as quickly as

possible, subject to funding, feasibility and statutory requirements. I would encourage

all Councillor and Oireachtas representatives to join in that effort.

Question submitted by Councillor E. O’Donovan

***I will ask the Mayor at the next Meeting of Limerick City and County Council of the feasibility***

***of Limerick Council purchasing the Sailors home on O’Curry Street to be used as a civic space,***

***as it is due to go for sale on the open market soon.***

**REPLY:** I thank Councillor O’Donovan for the question.

I fully agree that the Sailors’ Home on O’Curry Street has the potential to serve as an

important civic and cultural space for the city.

This objective is explicitly reflected in my Mayoral Programme, Objective ML102,

where I express the desire to:

“ML102 – Working with Limerick and Foynes Port Company have ensured the

redevelopment of the Ballantyne Mills as new office space and the restoration of the

Sailors Home as a new cultural facility”.

This is especially important timing with the planning permission for the adjacent

Gasworks site.

Since my election, and recognising that restoration works were ongoing, I have

continued to explore the feasibility of this objective.

In that context, I have had informal discussions at the margins of other meetings with

both the Chair and the Chief Executive of Shannon Foynes Port Company to indicate

potential interest on my part, should the property be made available for sale, or

alternatively if a lease or deferred purchase arrangement were to be considered. Such

an approach could, subject to agreement, facilitate an extension of the overall project.
---
14

If Shannon Foynes Port Company were to decide of its interest in the Sailors’ Home, it

is my understanding that, as a State Body, it would be required to comply with Circular

11/15: *Protocols for the Transfer and Sharing of State Property Assets*.

Under those protocols, any proposed disposal would first have to be offered to other

State bodies, and the valuation would be determined by the Valuation Office.
