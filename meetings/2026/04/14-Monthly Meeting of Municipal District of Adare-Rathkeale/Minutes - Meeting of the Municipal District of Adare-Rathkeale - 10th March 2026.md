**I will ask at the next Meeting for a detailed guideline from our motor tax section as to**

**how an individual obtains a registration or the process involved in registering a classic**

**or vintage vehicle and how an individual can register a vehicle in his or her name with**

**just the vehicles registration number.**

**Reply:**

**Classification:**

Any vehicle body type 30 years old from date of manufacture qualifies as

vintage on which a concessionary rate of motor tax can apply.

**Declaration:**

Assuming the vehicle in question is already recorded on the National

Vehicle and Driver File (NVDF), changing the taxation class on any vehicle

eligible for vintage classification, requires a declaration on section 4 of

application form RF111 (change of particulars)

https://www.motortax.ie/OMT/pdf/RF111\_en.pdf

and the submission of the old VRC (Vehicle Registration Certificate).

If the vehicle was registered in the Republic of Ireland prior to 1993 and

the Registration (Log) Book is available, the Registration Book needs to be

submitted with the RF111 form. The Registration Book will be updated to

“vintage” and returned to the registered owner.

**Other considerations:**

Where the vehicle has been acquired without the appropriate registration

documentation the new owner should:

**If NOT the registered owner of a vehicle registered in the Republic of**

**Ireland prior to 1993.**

• Submit a completed Statutory Declaration. This form can be

obtained by contacting the local Motor Tax Office.

• If registered as owner but has no Registration Book, complete an

RF134 application and submit with €12.00 fee for a replacement

Registration (Log) Book.

• If the details of the vehicle on the NVDF do not correspond to what

the individual has submitted, then the individual has no option but

to re-register their vehicle with the National Car Testing Service

(NCTS). I understand the fee for this is €200 and the NCTS will

issue either a “ZV” number or a registration number in the format

of 72-LK-1234 for example, if the vehicle was originally

manufactured in 1972.

• If the vehicle details are not on the NVDF, but the person in

possession of the vehicle has a registration number for the vehicle,
---
8

we can check with the relevant Motor Tax Office where the vehicle

was originally registered. “IU” vehicles were originally registered in

Limerick. “IN” vehicles originally registered in Clare etc. The

availability of those records (and if the details correspond) will

determine if the application can be processed.

**If NOT the registered owner of a vehicle registered in the Republic of**

**Ireland after 1993.**

• These vehicles hold a Vehicle Registration Certificate (VRC), and the

customer needs to contact the Change of Ownership Section,

Department of Transport, Shannon, County Clare as there is a

separate Statutory Declaration Form for those vehicles.

• 0818-411-412 and dvcsdchangeofownership@transport.gov.ie are

the contact details for the office in Shannon.

For individual queries, the local Motor Tax Office can also be contacted as

**I will ask at the next Meeting how many wind farm noise and shadow flicker**

**Reply:**

There is currently one planning enforcement case regarding wind farm

**Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor A. Teskey**

**I will move at the next Meeting that Limerick City and County Council undertake a**

**review of its current procedures for the assessment of complaints relating to wind**

**turbine noise and shadow flicker.**

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Teskey, seconded by Councillor Ward

and agreed.

In proposing the Motion Councillor Teskey stated given the amount of windfarm

installations in this Country, households do not feel they are better off financially with
---
10

the current infrastructure and current energy costs. He also noted that a lot of wind

turbines have stopped producing energy as the national grid currently does not have the

capacity to store the electricity produced. He asked how many open complaints have

been received by both the Planning and Environment Departments of Limerick City and

County Council for noise and shadow flicker in West Limerick region and stated where a

person’s health was put at risk due to noise and shadow flicker, questioned if these

complaints should be referred to the Health Service Executive for investigation. The

Administrative Officer, Development Management & Place-Making advised Councillor

Teskey the Health Service Executive do not have the tools or guidance to investigate

such complaints. She added 1 noise and 3 shadow flicker complaints are currently being

investigated. Councillor Teskey asked for a Council staff technical attendee at the April

meeting advising what Limerick City and County Councils policy is upon receipt of a noise

complaint in a particular area and what technical equipment they use to investigate

same.

The Director of Housing noted that some of the questions being asked were beyond the

wording of the Notice of Motion submitted and asked Councillor Teskey if he wished to

amend his Notice of Motion. Councillor Teskey stated he would need to find out what

the current procedures are adding a letter should be forwarded to the National Planning

Regulator stating that the applicant cannot be the sole supplier of information in

relation to shadow flicker and noise, this should be carried out by an independent third

party. Councillor Teskey concluded that he had been informed that Offaly County

Council have formally agreed to refer complaints such as shadow flicker and noise to the

Health Service Executive who have agreed to engage with this Council on these issues.

He asked that the Director of Planning for Offaly County Council be contacted to

ascertain what procedures are used when dealing with these type of complaints.

**Reply:**

Where a complaint concerns wind turbine noise and shadow flicker

associated with a planning permission and any associated conditions, the

Planning Enforcement Team, in conjunction with the Environment Team

where necessary, investigate complaints in line with the requirements of

the Planning and Development Act 2000 (as amended). A review of

current procedures will be undertaken.
