**Regeneration, Community, Sports & Rural Development Directorate**

**Part 8 Procedure - Kilteery**

Circulated, Mayor’s Report, dated 21st May, 2026 setting out details in relation to the

proposal for the development at Kilteery Pier. The Report had been prepared in

accordance with Section 179 of the Planning and Development Act, 2000 (as amended)

and Part VIII of the Planning and Development Regulations, 2001 (as amended).

The Town Regeneration Officer, Rural Development addressed the Meeting and advised

Members the report as presented requires to be either approved, amended or rejected.

Councillor Teskey proposed a five minute adjournment to allow Members a private

discussion on this item. This was seconded by Councillor Collins and agreed.

On resumption of the Meeting Members raised the following issues with the report, as

presented:

• Councillor O’Donoghue asked if it was possible to relocate the proposed structure

to an alternative area within the pier and also expressed concern in relation to

future maintenance costs for the upkeep of this proposed structure. He further

stated that he would like to see a public convenience operational for the three

months of the year that the lifeguards are present at this site.

• Councillor Keary expressed reservations on the proposed project and requested

additional funding be sought to amend the Part VIII plans to include the

installation of a wheelchair accessible self cleaning toilet unit.

• Councillor Collins asked what the spend to date for this project has been and

expressed concern that if this report was rejected the funding might not be

available to bring a new Part VIII procedure before Members in the future.

• Councillor Teskey requested the costings associated should a new Part VIII

project be sought to include Members amendments and concerns.

The Town Regeneration Officer, Rural Development advised Members the toilet facilities

provided for the lifeguards is universally accessible and this facility could be examined

with a view to allowing pubic access on a trial basis, however, this request would also

come with additional operational costs. Members queried what costs would be

involved. The Town Regeneration Officer, Rural Development advised the toilet facility

would have waste storage capacity which would be cleaned and removed to a waste

water treatment plant facility and would require a financial budget to maintain the

waste facility. Discussion ensued in relation to public lighting at the location and the

lighting is not included due to the facility being used during day light hours. Further

July, 2026.

**Notice of Motion submitted by Councillor S. Keary**

**I will move at the next Meeting that on the grounds of Public Health and Safety that**

**Limerick City and County Council would immediately conduct a structural survey on**

**the protected structure known as Riddlestown Park at Riddlestown, Rathkeale, Co.**

**Limerick. Record of Protected Structures Record Number 665. National Inventory of**

**Architectural Heritage 21901919 with a view to having same removed from the said**

**registers.**

The Notice of Motion was proposed by Councillor Keary, seconded by Councillor Teskey

and agreed.

In proposing the Motion Councillor Keary stated this property is in an extremely

dangerous condition and the owner of the property has been prohibited from reroofing

it and reinstating the windows as well as been constrained from carrying out work on

farm buildings in the vicinity of the property. He added the owner should be allowed to

demolish the house as the cost to repair it would be in excess of €2.5 million.

The Senior Executive Engineer, Environment, Rural Water and Climate Action advised as

her department are working within the confines of the Sanitary Services Act they are

very limited in what they can do as there is no immediate risk to public safety. She

further advised that she would speak to Shona O’Keeffe, Conservation Officer, Limerick

City and County Council to have the building delisted.

The Meetings Administrator advised she had received correspondence from Maria

Woods, Senior Planner, Limerick City and County Council stating that there is no basis at

this time for the removal of Riddlestown Park from the Record of Protected Structures.

The Conservation Section are happy to engage with the owners in relation to any

structural or conservation assessments required, the identification of appropriate works,

and any available conservation grant supports.
---
5

Councillor Teskey added that the owners of this property are financially unable to carry

out the works required to bring this property back into a habitable state and felt that the

existing laws around the upgrading of listed building needed to change to prevent this

from happening to owners of listed properties in the future.

Councillor Collins asked what the process is for de registering a building. The Director

advised the officer in charge of this process is responsible for determining if a building

should be de registered, if it is recommended it must then be referred to Full Council for

a vote. Councillor Keary asked that this Motion would be forwarded to Full Council for

consideration. The Director advised that the Motion should be forwarded to Shona

O’Keeffe, Conservation Officer for her recommendation in the first instance.

**Reply:**

The Dangerous Structures Team carried out an inspection of Riddlestown

House on 15th October 2025. At the time of inspection, the external fabric

of the structure was generally in good condition. While there has been a

partial internal collapse of the roof structure, this appears to be due to a

lack of maintenance and weatherproofing and has not impacted the

stability of the external supporting walls. The property is located

approximately 450 metres from the nearest public road, and therefore the

potential risk to public health and safety arising from its current condition

is considered to be extremely low.

The Council can request the owner to undertake a structural survey where

there are concerns about the integrity of the structure or a risk to the

public. This is not the case here.

Based on the findings of the inspection, there is no identified requirement

to carry out a structural survey or to pursue demolition of this protected

structure.
